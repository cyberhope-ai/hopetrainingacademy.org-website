How the field grew

1940sPost-World War II healthcare expansion led to increased administrative burdens on nursing staff.
1960sThe role of the 'ward clerk' or 'unit secretary' emerged to manage non-clinical tasks, freeing nurses for patient care.
1980sIntroduction of early computer systems in hospitals began to transform administrative workflows.
2000sThe widespread adoption of Electronic Health Records (EHRs) solidified the Health Unit Coordinator's role as a key player in digital information management.

What the career pays

Health Unit Coordinators are essential administrative professionals who manage information, communication, and patient records within medical units. They ensure the efficient delivery of health care by supporting medical staff and facilitating patient flow.

$30,000entry
$39,030median
$50,000+experienced

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (SOC 43-6013, Medical Secretaries and Administrative Assistants)

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a college degree to enroll?

No, this program is designed for individuals seeking direct entry into a healthcare administrative role, with no prior degree required.

Is the NAHUC certification exam voucher included?

An exam voucher for the NAHUC certification is not included with this training program.

How long will it take to complete the program?

This program consists of 120 course hours and is self-paced, allowing you to complete it according to your schedule.

Which certification exam does this program prepare me for?

This program prepares you to sit for the National Association of Health Unit Coordinators (NAHUC) certification exam.

What kind of job can I get after completing this program?

Upon completion, you'll be prepared for roles such as a Health Unit Coordinator, Medical Administrative Assistant, or Ward Clerk in hospitals, clinics, and various medical offices.
