What is this field?

A manual machinist is a skilled craftsperson who uses traditional machine tools like lathes, mills, and grinders to shape metal and other materials with extreme precision. This hands-on career training program equips you with the foundational knowledge and practical skills essential for creating critical components used across various industries, from aerospace to medical devices.

Think of a manual machinist as a highly precise sculptor, but instead of clay, they work with robust materials, guided by blueprints and exacting specifications. Our program enhances this learning with immersive virtual reality (VR) labs and simulations, utilizing a Meta Quest 3 headset to provide a dynamic and interactive training experience.

How the field grew

1770sInvention of the first precision boring machine, a foundational step for industrial machining.
1800sDevelopment of interchangeable parts and standardized manufacturing, heavily reliant on skilled machinists.
1950sIntroduction of Computer Numerical Control (CNC) technology, beginning the integration of digital precision with traditional methods.
TodayModern manufacturing demands machinists who can operate both manual and increasingly automated machinery, often leveraging virtual reality for advanced training.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Foundational Machining Skills

Production floor safety, workholding principles, basic math for machining, material properties, quality fundamentals.

02
Precision Grinding Operations

Grinding safety, basic grinding theory, surface, cylindrical, and centerless grinder operation, grinding materials, dressing and truing.

03
Manual Machine Tool Operation & Assembly

Manual mill basics, engine lathe basics, fastener ergonomics, threaded and non-threaded fasteners, understanding torque.

04
Technical Measurement & Inspection

Basic measurement, calibration, tolerance, blueprint reading, GD&T, hole and thread inspection, CMMs and optical comparators.

05
Introduction to CNC Technology

Basics of CNC lathes and mills, coordinates, G-code programming, CAD/CAM concepts, control panel functions (Fanuc, Haas, Mazak).

06
Manufacturing Optimization Principles

Lean manufacturing, 5S overview, Six Sigma introduction, Total Productive Maintenance (TPM), Statistical Process Control (SPC).
