What is this field?

Bloom's Taxonomy is a foundational framework in education, classifying learning objectives into a hierarchy of cognitive skills. Developed by Benjamin Bloom in the 1950s and later revised, it provides educators with a powerful tool to design curriculum, instruction, and assessments that challenge students to move beyond simple recall to more complex thought processes like analysis, evaluation, and creation.

Think of Bloom's Taxonomy as a staircase for learning. Instead of just asking students to memorize facts (the bottom step), this framework helps you guide them up the stairs to understand why those facts matter, apply them in new situations, break them down into components, judge their value, and even create something new from them. It ensures your lessons are not just about 'what,' but also 'how' and 'why,' leading to more profound and lasting comprehension.

How the field grew

1956Original Taxonomy of Educational Objectives published by Benjamin Bloom and collaborators.
1990sRevision process initiated, led by Lorin Anderson and David Krathwohl, a former student of Bloom.
2001A Taxonomy for Teaching, Learning, and Assessing: A Revision of Bloom's Taxonomy of Educational Objectives published, introducing a two-dimensional framework.
