What is this field?

The Science of Reading is a vast body of research, spanning decades and multiple disciplines like cognitive psychology, linguistics, and neuroscience, that explains how humans learn to read. It synthesizes findings on effective reading instruction, debunking myths and providing clear, evidence-based practices to teach reading successfully.

Think of it like understanding the 'owner's manual' for the human brain's reading circuitry. Instead of guessing how to fix a complex machine, the Science of Reading provides proven blueprints and tools to build strong literacy skills, ensuring every learner has the best chance to become a proficient reader.

How the field grew

1970sEmergence of cognitive psychology's influence on reading research, shifting focus to internal mental processes.
1986Publication of 'Becoming a Nation of Readers,' a landmark report emphasizing the importance of both phonics and comprehension.
2000The National Reading Panel Report synthesizes decades of research, identifying five critical components of effective reading instruction.
2010s-PresentRenewed focus and widespread adoption of evidence-based reading practices, often driven by legislative initiatives and educational reforms.
