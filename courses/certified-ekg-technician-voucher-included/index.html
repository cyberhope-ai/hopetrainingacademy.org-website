What is this field?

An EKG (electrocardiogram) technician is a vital healthcare professional who performs diagnostic tests to assess the electrical activity of a patient's heart. These tests help physicians diagnose and monitor various heart conditions, from arrhythmias to heart attacks. EKG technicians work closely with patients, ensuring their comfort and safety while accurately recording critical cardiac data.

Think of an EKG as a 'heart map' – it traces the electrical signals that make your heart beat, much like a seismograph records earthquake tremors. An EKG technician is the skilled cartographer who creates this map, providing doctors with crucial information to understand the heart's rhythm and function.

How the field grew

1903Willem Einthoven invents the first practical electrocardiograph (ECG).
1924Einthoven receives the Nobel Prize for his groundbreaking work on the ECG mechanism.
Mid-20th CenturyECG becomes a standard and indispensable diagnostic tool in hospitals worldwide.
Late 20th CenturyAdvancements lead to digital EKG machines, enhancing portability and data analysis capabilities.

What the career pays

EKG technicians operate specialized equipment to record and analyze the electrical impulses of the heart, assisting physicians in diagnosing cardiovascular conditions. They prepare patients for procedures, ensure accurate test results, and maintain equipment in various healthcare settings.
