What is this field?

Administrative assistants are the backbone of any successful organization, performing a wide array of clerical and administrative duties necessary to keep an office running smoothly. Their responsibilities often include managing schedules, preparing documents, handling communications, and organizing records, ensuring that professionals can focus on their core tasks without interruption.

Think of an administrative assistant as the air traffic controller of an office. Just as an air traffic controller ensures planes land, take off, and navigate safely and efficiently, an administrative assistant orchestrates the flow of information, tasks, and schedules within an organization, preventing bottlenecks and ensuring everyone stays on course towards their objectives.

How the field grew

1870sThe invention of the typewriter creates new demand for 'typewriters,' marking the beginning of formalized administrative roles.
Early 1900sThe role of 'secretary' becomes prominent, evolving into a professional position focused on correspondence, record-keeping, and office management.
1950s-1960sIntroduction of office automation like photocopiers and early computing systems begins to transform administrative tasks, requiring new technical skills.
1970s-1980sThe title shifts from 'secretary' to 'administrative assistant,' reflecting a broader scope of responsibilities and increased professionalization.
2000s-PresentIntegration of advanced digital tools, cloud-based software, and virtual communication necessitates strong technological proficiency and adaptability in the modern administrative role.
