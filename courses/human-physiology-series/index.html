What is this field?

Human physiology is the scientific study of the mechanical, physical, and biochemical functions of humans in good health, their organs, and the cells of which they are composed. It delves into how our bodies operate, from the smallest cellular processes to the complex interactions between organ systems that allow us to move, think, and live. Understanding physiology is crucial for anyone pursuing a career in healthcare, fitness, or biological research.

Think of your body as a highly sophisticated machine, like a complex car. While anatomy describes the parts (the engine, wheels, steering wheel), physiology explains how those parts work together to make the car run, steer, and stop (how the engine converts fuel to motion, how the steering wheel controls direction). This course provides the owner's manual for the human body, detailing how each system contributes to overall health and function.

How the field grew

Ancient GreeceEarly observations by Hippocrates and Galen laid the groundwork for understanding bodily functions.
1628William Harvey's discovery of blood circulation revolutionized the understanding of the cardiovascular system.
19th CenturyClaude Bernard introduced the concept of homeostasis, emphasizing the body's ability to maintain internal stability.
20th CenturyAdvances in molecular biology and genetics deepened understanding of physiological processes at the cellular level.
