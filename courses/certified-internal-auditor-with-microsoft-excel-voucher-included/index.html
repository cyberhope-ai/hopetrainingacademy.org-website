What is this field?

Internal auditors are the guardians of an organization's operational efficiency and financial integrity. They evaluate risks, ensure compliance with policies and regulations, and recommend improvements to processes and controls. Think of them as an organization's internal compass, constantly checking that everything is running smoothly and ethically, preventing potential issues before they escalate.

In this vital role, proficiency in data analysis is paramount. Microsoft Excel serves as an indispensable tool, allowing internal auditors to efficiently manage, analyze, and visualize complex financial and operational data. From tracking expenditures to identifying anomalies, Excel empowers auditors to uncover insights, present findings clearly, and support strategic decision-making, making their work more precise and impactful.

How the field grew

1941The Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA) is founded, establishing a professional body for internal audit.
1974The Certified Internal Auditor (CIA) designation is introduced by the IIA, setting a global standard for the profession.
1985Microsoft Excel makes its debut on the Macintosh, revolutionizing spreadsheet software.
1987Microsoft Excel is released for Windows, quickly becoming an industry standard for data analysis.

Is a college degree required to become a Certified Internal Auditor?

While a bachelor's degree is generally the entry-level education for accountants and auditors, the Certified Internal Auditor (CIA) certification has specific eligibility requirements for education, work experience, and character that candidates must meet, which may include a degree or equivalent professional experience.
