What is this field?

Differentiated instruction is a teaching approach that customizes curriculum and teaching methods to meet the individual needs of all students in a classroom. Rather than a one-size-fits-all model, it involves proactively planning varied approaches to content, process, and product to address differences in students' readiness, interests, and learning profiles.

Think of it like a master chef preparing a meal for a diverse group of diners. Instead of serving the exact same dish to everyone, the chef adjusts ingredients, cooking methods, and presentation to cater to different dietary restrictions, preferences, and appetites, ensuring everyone enjoys a satisfying and nourishing experience. Similarly, differentiated instruction ensures every student receives the 'meal' of education in a way that best suits their unique learning needs.

How the field grew

1970sEmergence of individualized learning concepts and student-centered approaches in education.
1990sCarol Ann Tomlinson's foundational work popularized and defined differentiated instruction as a comprehensive teaching philosophy.
Early 2000sWidespread adoption and integration of differentiated instruction into educational policy and teacher training programs.
CurrentContinued evolution with digital tools and a focus on equity, inclusion, and personalized learning for all students.
