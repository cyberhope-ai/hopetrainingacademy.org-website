What is this field?

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition that impacts millions of students, affecting their attention, impulse control, and activity levels. Understanding ADHD goes beyond recognizing common behaviors; it involves appreciating the unique ways these students process information and interact with their learning environment. This course provides educators with the knowledge and tools to create supportive and effective classroom settings.

Imagine a student trying to focus on a single task while their brain is constantly processing multiple streams of information, like trying to read a book in a busy, noisy room. This course helps you understand that internal experience and equip you with practical, research-backed strategies to help students navigate their learning journey more successfully, ensuring they can thrive academically and socially.

How the field grew

1902Sir George Still describes 'defective moral control' in children, an early recognition of ADHD-like symptoms.
1960sThe term 'hyperkinetic impulse disorder' emerges, focusing on hyperactivity as a key characteristic.
1980The DSM-III introduces 'Attention Deficit Disorder (ADD),' distinguishing between those with and without hyperactivity.
1987The DSM-III-R renames the condition to 'Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD),' emphasizing both components.
1994The DSM-IV defines three distinct presentations of ADHD: predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, and combined type.
