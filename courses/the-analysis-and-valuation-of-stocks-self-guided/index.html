What is this field?

Stock analysis and valuation involve meticulously examining a company's financial health, industry position, and future prospects to determine the true worth of its shares. This process helps investors make informed decisions, identifying undervalued assets with growth potential or overvalued ones to avoid. It's about understanding the fundamental drivers behind a stock's price, not just reacting to market fluctuations.

Imagine you're buying a house. You wouldn't just look at the asking price; you'd investigate its foundation, neighborhood, comparable sales, and future development potential. Stock valuation is similar – it's about looking beyond the surface market price to understand a company's intrinsic value and its long-term investment viability.

How the field grew

1800sEarly forms of financial reporting and basic stock trading emerge.
1934Securities Exchange Act establishes the SEC, increasing transparency in financial markets.
1950s-70sGrowth of fundamental analysis (Graham & Dodd) and quantitative investment methods.
1980s-90sDiscounted Cash Flow (DCF) models become prevalent for valuing companies.
2000s-PresentDigitalization, algorithmic trading, and big data analytics transform valuation techniques.
