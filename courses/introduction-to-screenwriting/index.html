What is this field?

Screenwriting is the art and craft of writing scripts for films, television shows, video games, and other visual media. It involves developing compelling narratives, creating memorable characters, crafting authentic dialogue, and structuring stories in a way that translates effectively to the screen.

Think of a screenplay as the blueprint for a house. It details every room, every wall, every window, but it's not the house itself. It's the essential guide that directors, actors, and crew use to build the final film or show, ensuring everyone works from a shared vision.

How the field grew

1895First public film screenings, laying the groundwork for visual storytelling.
1927Introduction of synchronized sound with 'The Jazz Singer,' making dialogue a central element of screenplays.
1941Orson Welles' 'Citizen Kane' pushes narrative boundaries and character depth in cinema.
1970sEmergence of the 'New Hollywood' era, emphasizing writer-director vision and complex narratives.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a college degree to become a screenwriter?

No, a college degree is not required. Industry professionals value strong portfolios and compelling scripts above formal education, focusing on your ability to tell a great story.

Does this program include an exam voucher?

This program focuses on skill development and the creation of a portfolio-ready script; it does not lead to an external certification or include an exam voucher.

How long does it take to complete the Introduction to Screenwriting program?

This is a self-paced online program, allowing you to progress at your own speed. While typical completion varies, you have ample time to master the material.

Is there a specific exam I need to pass for screenwriting?

Screenwriting success is measured by the quality of your script, your storytelling ability, and your professional network, rather than by passing a standardized exam.

What kind of jobs can I get after completing this program?

Graduates can pursue roles such as screenwriter for film or television, script editor, story developer, or content creator for various media production companies.
