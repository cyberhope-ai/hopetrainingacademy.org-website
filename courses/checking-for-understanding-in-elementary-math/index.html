What is this field?

Checking for Understanding in Elementary Math is a vital skill for K-6 educators, enabling them to gauge student comprehension in real-time and adapt instruction accordingly. This program equips teachers with practical strategies to foster a supportive learning environment, ensuring every student feels confident exploring mathematical concepts.

Imagine teaching a complex math problem, and instead of just asking 'Does everyone get it?', you have a toolkit of techniques to truly see where each student stands. This program helps you move beyond surface-level answers to uncover the root of difficulties, much like a detective uses clues to solve a mystery, but for math learning.

How the field grew

Early PedagogyFocus on direct instruction and rote memorization in math education.
Mid-20th CenturyEmergence of constructivist theories, emphasizing student-centered learning and active knowledge construction.
Late 20th CenturyDevelopment of formative assessment strategies to monitor student learning and guide instruction.
21st CenturyIntegration of data-driven teaching and differentiated instruction to address diverse learning needs and common misconceptions.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Module 1: Building a Positive Math Classroom Culture

Strategies for fostering engagement, promoting a growth mindset, and creating a safe learning space for K-6 math.

02
Module 2: Effective Techniques for Checking Understanding

Implementing formative assessment, active listening, and strategic questioning in elementary math instruction.

03
Module 3: Identifying Common K-6 Math Misconceptions

Analyzing student errors, understanding developmental stages, and recognizing typical pitfalls in elementary math concepts.

04
Module 4: Addressing Misconceptions and Differentiated Instruction

Developing targeted interventions, re-teaching strategies, and tailoring instruction to meet diverse student needs in math.

05
Module 5: Integrating Assessment and Responsive Teaching

Using data to inform instructional decisions and continuously improve teaching practices in the elementary math classroom.
