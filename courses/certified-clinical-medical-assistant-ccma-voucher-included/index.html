What is this field?

A Certified Clinical Medical Assistant (CCMA) is a vital, multi-skilled healthcare professional who supports physicians and other medical staff in various clinical and administrative tasks. They are the backbone of efficient patient care, ensuring smooth operations from the front office to the exam room. Think of a CCMA as the central nervous system of a medical practice, coordinating patient flow, managing records, and providing direct patient support to keep everything running seamlessly.

CCMAs perform a wide range of duties, including taking vital signs, assisting with examinations, performing basic lab tests, administering injections, and managing patient records and scheduling. Their expertise allows healthcare facilities to operate more efficiently, leading to better patient experiences and improved overall productivity. This comprehensive role makes them indispensable members of any healthcare team.

How the field grew

1960sThe role of medical assistants began to formalize and expand in response to growing healthcare demands.
1989The National Healthcareer Association (NHA) was founded, establishing certification pathways for various allied health professions, including medical assisting.
Early 2000sThe Certified Clinical Medical Assistant (CCMA) credential gained prominence as a recognized standard for demonstrating competency in clinical and administrative medical assisting skills.
Present DayCCMAs are increasingly sought after, with certification becoming a key indicator of a medical assistant's professional readiness and commitment to quality patient care.
