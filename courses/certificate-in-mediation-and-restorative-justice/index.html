What is this field?

Mediation and restorative justice are powerful approaches to resolving conflicts and repairing harm. Mediation involves a neutral third party guiding disputing parties toward a mutually acceptable agreement, focusing on communication and common ground. Restorative justice, on the other hand, centers on repairing harm caused by crime or conflict, involving victims, offenders, and communities in a process to address needs and responsibilities.

Think of it like a skilled facilitator who helps people navigate a complex maze. Instead of telling them which path to take, you provide tools and guidance, empowering them to find their own way out, rebuild trust, and move forward constructively.

How the field grew

1976The Pound Conference highlights the need for Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) in the U.S. legal system.
1980sGrowth of community mediation centers across the United States, offering accessible conflict resolution services.
1990sFederal legislation encourages the use of mediation and arbitration in federal courts and agencies.
Early 2000sIncreased adoption of restorative justice practices in schools, communities, and the criminal justice system.
