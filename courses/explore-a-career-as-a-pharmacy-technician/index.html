What is this field?

A pharmacy technician is a vital member of the healthcare team, working alongside pharmacists to dispense medications, manage inventory, and assist patients. They play a crucial role in ensuring that individuals receive the correct prescriptions safely and efficiently, supporting the overall operations of pharmacies in various settings.

Think of a pharmacy technician as the air traffic controller for medications. Just as air traffic controllers ensure planes land and take off safely and on schedule, pharmacy technicians meticulously manage the flow of prescriptions, verifying details, preparing doses, and organizing stock to ensure patients get the right medication at the right time, every time.

How the field grew

1950sInformal roles for pharmacy assistants begin to emerge in hospitals and community pharmacies.
1970sThe role gains more recognition, leading to the development of early, structured training programs.
1995The Pharmacy Technician Certification Board (PTCB) is established, introducing the first national certification exam.
2000sIncreased state-level regulation and licensure requirements standardize the profession across the United States.
PresentPharmacy technicians take on expanded responsibilities, including medication reconciliation and immunization support, under pharmacist supervision.

What the career pays

Pharmacy technicians assist licensed pharmacists in dispensing prescription medications, managing patient records, and maintaining pharmacy inventory. They are crucial for efficient pharmacy operations and ensuring patient safety by accurately preparing and labeling medications.
