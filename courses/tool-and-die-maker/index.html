What is this field?

Tool and Die Makers are highly skilled artisans who are indispensable to modern manufacturing. They specialize in designing, building, and repairing precision tools, dies, jigs, and fixtures that are used to create parts and products across various industries, from automotive to aerospace and medical devices. Their expertise ensures the accuracy, efficiency, and quality of mass-produced components.

Think of a Tool and Die Maker as the master architect and builder of the factory's most crucial instruments. Just as a chef meticulously crafts the perfect mold for a culinary masterpiece, these professionals meticulously engineer and fabricate the molds, stamps, and cutting tools that shape raw materials into finished goods. Our program uniquely integrates virtual reality (VR) technology, providing immersive, hands-on experience in a simulated manufacturing environment to hone these critical skills.

How the field grew

18th CenturyThe Industrial Revolution drives the need for skilled artisans to create precision tools and molds for manufacturing.
Early 20th CenturyMass production techniques emerge, making standardized dies and tooling indispensable for industries like automotive and consumer goods.
Late 20th CenturyComputer Numerical Control (CNC) technology transforms the craft, allowing for greater precision and automation in tool and die production.
Present DayModern tool and die makers integrate advanced CAD/CAM software, robotics, and new materials to meet complex manufacturing demands.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Foundational Principles

Introduction to workholding, essential math fundamentals, algebra, geometry, and blueprint reading.

02
Precision Measurement & Quality

Basic measurement techniques, Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T), and an introduction to quality management systems like Lean and Six Sigma.

03
Machining & Metalworking

Metal cutting processes, manual mill and lathe operations, basic cutting theory, and an introduction to metallurgy.

04
Advanced CNC Operations

Introduction to CNC machines, coordinates, G-code programming, CAD/CAM basics, and control panel functions for various CNC systems (Haas, Fanuc, Mazak).

05
Workplace Safety & Materials

OSHA standards, personal protective equipment, lockout/tagout procedures, material properties, classification of steel, and heat treatment essentials.

06
Manufacturing Excellence

Lean manufacturing overview, continuous process improvement, 5S methodology, Total Productive Maintenance (TPM), and leadership skills.
