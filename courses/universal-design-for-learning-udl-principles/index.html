What is this field?

Universal Design for Learning (UDL) is a framework that guides the design of learning environments to make them accessible and engaging for all individuals. It emphasizes creating flexible learning experiences from the outset, rather than retrofitting accommodations, ensuring that diverse learners can access and participate in education effectively.

Think of UDL like designing a building with a ramp. While initially intended for wheelchair users, that ramp also benefits parents with strollers, delivery personnel, and anyone with temporary mobility issues. Similarly, UDL designs lessons with multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression, benefiting students with varying learning styles, abilities, and backgrounds, not just those with identified special needs.

How the field grew

1984CAST (Center for Applied Special Technology) founded, pioneering UDL research.
1990sThe UDL framework is formalized, emphasizing flexible learning environments.
2008Higher Education Opportunity Act (HEOA) includes a federal definition of UDL.
2015Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) promotes UDL for K-12 education.
