What is this field?

Corporate accounting is the systematic recording, analyzing, and reporting of financial transactions for a business or organization. It's the backbone of financial health, providing crucial insights into a company's performance, profitability, and stability. From managing daily transactions to preparing comprehensive financial statements, corporate accounting ensures transparency and compliance, guiding strategic decisions.

Think of corporate accounting as the financial GPS for a business. Just as a GPS tracks your location, speed, and destination to help you navigate, corporate accounting tracks a company's money, resources, and obligations to help it navigate the economic landscape. It tells stakeholders where the company has been, where it is now, and helps predict where it's headed financially.

How the field grew

1494Luca Pacioli publishes 'Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni et Proportionalita,' detailing double-entry bookkeeping, a foundational accounting principle still used today.
1887The American Association of Public Accountants is formed, marking the beginning of organized professional accounting in the U.S.
1934The Securities Exchange Act is passed in the U.S., establishing the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and mandating financial reporting for publicly traded companies, significantly shaping corporate accounting practices.
1973The Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) is established, becoming the primary organization for setting generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) in the U.S.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Understanding Merchandising Operations

Explore accounting for sales revenue, cost of goods sold, and inventory in merchandising businesses.

02
Inventory Management and Valuation

Learn different inventory costing methods (FIFO, LIFO, weighted-average) and their impact on financial statements.

03
Cash and Internal Controls

Examine the importance of cash management, bank reconciliations, and internal control systems to safeguard assets.

04
Accounting for Receivables and Payables

Master the principles of managing accounts receivable, estimating uncollectible accounts, and handling current liabilities.

05
Plant Assets, Natural Resources, and Intangible Assets

Understand depreciation, depletion, amortization, and accounting for long-term assets.

06
Corporate Accounting for Equity

Delve into the accounting for corporations, including stock issuance, dividends, and retained earnings.

07
Statement of Cash Flows

Learn to prepare and analyze the statement of cash flows, distinguishing between operating, investing, and financing activities.

08
Financial Statement Analysis

Develop skills to interpret financial statements using ratio analysis to assess a company's liquidity, solvency, and profitability.
