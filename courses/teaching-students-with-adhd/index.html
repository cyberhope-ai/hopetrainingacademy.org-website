What is this field?

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition affecting millions of children, impacting their ability to focus, control impulses, and manage activity levels. In the classroom, this can manifest as challenges with organization, completing tasks, following instructions, and maintaining attention, often leading to frustration for both students and educators.

This training program equips educators with the essential tools and understanding to navigate these challenges effectively. Think of it like learning to adjust the sails on a boat to catch the wind just right, rather than fighting against the current. By understanding the unique cognitive profiles of students with ADHD, you can implement tailored strategies that transform potential obstacles into pathways for academic and social success.

How the field grew

1980ADHD first recognized as a distinct diagnostic category in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-III).
1990Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) ensures free appropriate public education for students with disabilities, including ADHD.
2000sIncreased research into executive function deficits and varied presentations of ADHD, leading to more nuanced support strategies.
PresentGrowing emphasis on multi-modal interventions, strength-based approaches, and inclusive classroom practices for students with ADHD.
