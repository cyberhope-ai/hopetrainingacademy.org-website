What is this field?

Logical fallacies are common errors in reasoning that undermine the logic of an argument, making it unsound or misleading. Understanding these flaws is crucial for anyone seeking to evaluate information critically, construct persuasive arguments, and make informed decisions. This course provides a foundational overview, equipping you with the tools to recognize and address these errors in both academic and everyday settings.

Think of logical fallacies like hidden trapdoors in an argument. Just as a trapdoor can lead to an unexpected fall, a logical fallacy can lead to a faulty conclusion, even if the initial premise seems plausible. By learning to spot these intellectual pitfalls, you become a more discerning listener and a more effective communicator, capable of navigating complex discussions with clarity and precision.

How the field grew

c. 350 BCEAristotle's 'Sophistical Refutations' provides the first systematic classification of logical fallacies.
1620Francis Bacon introduces the concept of 'Idols of the Mind' in 'Novum Organum,' highlighting sources of human error.
1843John Stuart Mill's 'A System of Logic' offers a comprehensive treatment and categorization of fallacies.
20th CenturyCritical thinking and formal logic become established academic disciplines, emphasizing fallacy identification.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Module 1: Introduction to Logical Fallacies

Define logical fallacies, understand their impact, and explore the importance of critical reasoning.

02
Module 2: Fallacies of Relevance

Examine common fallacies like Ad Hominem, Appeal to Authority, Red Herring, and Appeal to Emotion.

03
Module 3: Fallacies of Weak Induction

Identify errors such as Hasty Generalization, Slippery Slope, Appeal to Ignorance, and False Cause.

04
Module 4: Fallacies of Ambiguity

Learn about fallacies arising from unclear language, including Equivocation, Amphiboly, and Composition/Division.

05
Module 5: Fallacies of Presumption

Analyze fallacies that rely on unwarranted assumptions, like Begging the Question, Complex Question, and False Dichotomy.

06
Module 6: Applying Fallacy Detection

Practice identifying and addressing logical fallacies in various contexts, strengthening your analytical skills.
