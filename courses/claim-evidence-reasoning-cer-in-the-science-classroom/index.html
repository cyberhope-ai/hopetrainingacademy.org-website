What is this field?

Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) is a powerful instructional framework that helps students develop scientific argumentation skills. It provides a structured approach for students to articulate their understanding of scientific phenomena by making a claim, supporting it with relevant evidence, and explaining the reasoning that connects the evidence to the claim. This process mirrors how real scientists communicate their findings and build knowledge.

Think of CER like building a strong legal case. The 'Claim' is your main argument or thesis – what you believe to be true. The 'Evidence' is the data, observations, or facts you present to back up your claim, much like a lawyer presents exhibits. Finally, the 'Reasoning' is your explanation of how and why your evidence supports your claim, connecting the dots and demonstrating the scientific principles at play.

How the field grew

1990sScience education reforms began emphasizing inquiry-based learning and critical thinking over rote memorization, setting the stage for structured argumentation.
2000sEducational researchers and practitioners developed frameworks like Argument-Driven Inquiry (ADI) and focused on explicit teaching of scientific argumentation.
2012The Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) were released, prominently featuring 'Engaging in Argument from Evidence' as one of the eight essential Scientific and Engineering Practices, solidifying the importance of frameworks like CER.
