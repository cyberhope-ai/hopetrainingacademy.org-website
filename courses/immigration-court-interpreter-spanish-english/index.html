What is this field?

An Immigration Court Interpreter serves as the crucial linguistic link between non-English speaking individuals and the immigration court system. They ensure that all parties, including respondents, judges, and attorneys, can understand and be understood during complex legal proceedings. This role demands not just fluency in two languages, but also a deep understanding of legal terminology, court protocols, and ethical standards to ensure fair and accurate communication.

Think of an Immigration Court Interpreter as a human bridge, allowing two people who speak different languages to communicate as if they were speaking directly to each other. In the high-stakes environment of immigration court, this bridge must be incredibly strong and precise, ensuring every word, nuance, and legal concept is conveyed accurately and impartially, without adding or omitting any information.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a degree to become an Immigration Court Interpreter?

While a bachelor's degree is common for interpreters and translators, this program does not require one. However, you must be natively fluent in both English and Spanish and are recommended to have one year of legal field experience prior to enrolling.

Is an exam voucher included with this program?

Information regarding an exam voucher is not available for this program. Currently, there is no single certification exam for immigration court interpreters; the path typically involves a screening test, training, final completion exam, and performance evaluation.

How long does it take to complete this training program?

This program consists of 200 course hours. You will have 9 months from your enrollment date to complete the self-paced curriculum.

Does this course prepare me for a specific certification exam?

No, there is currently no single, official certification exam for immigration court interpreters. This program prepares you with the knowledge and skills required for the various screening and evaluation processes typically involved in becoming an immigration court interpreter, including a final completion examination.

What kind of job can I get after completing this program?

Upon successful completion, you will be prepared for roles such as an Immigration Court Interpreter or a Legal Interpreter. This training equips you with the specialized skills needed to work in immigration courts and other legal settings requiring bilingual communication.
