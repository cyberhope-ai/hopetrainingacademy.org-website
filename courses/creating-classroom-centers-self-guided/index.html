What is this field?

Classroom centers are thoughtfully designed learning stations within an educational environment, allowing students to engage in independent or small-group activities. These centers foster self-directed exploration, reinforce concepts, and develop critical skills, moving beyond whole-group instruction.

This program equips educators with the strategies to design, implement, and manage effective classroom centers. You'll learn to create engaging, easy-to-maintain activities that not only boost independent learning but also free up valuable time for small-group instruction and individualized attention.

How the field grew

Early 1900sMaria Montessori introduces the 'prepared environment' concept, emphasizing child-led learning stations.
1960s-1970sThe 'open classroom' movement popularizes flexible learning spaces and activity centers.
1980s-1990sIncreased focus on differentiated instruction drives the adoption of varied learning centers to meet diverse student needs.
TodayModern educational practices integrate technology and personalized learning into dynamic classroom centers.

The skills the work is built on

  • Active Listening: Giving full attention to what others are saying.
  • Speaking: Talking to others to convey information effectively.
  • Instructing: Teaching others how to do something.
  • Learning Strategies: Using and selecting instructional methods and procedures appropriate for the situation when learning or teaching new things.
  • Monitoring: Assessing performance of yourself, other individuals, or organizations to make improvements or take corrective action.
  • Social Perceptiveness: Being aware of others' reactions and understanding why they react as they do.
