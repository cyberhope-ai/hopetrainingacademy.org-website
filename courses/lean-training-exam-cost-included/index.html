What is this field?

Lean is a powerful methodology focused on maximizing customer value while minimizing waste. It's about identifying and eliminating non-value-adding activities in any process, from manufacturing to service delivery, to create more efficient and effective workflows. By fostering a culture of continuous improvement, Lean empowers teams to streamline operations, reduce costs, and enhance overall quality.

Think of Lean like decluttering and organizing your kitchen. You identify what you use regularly, remove unnecessary items, and arrange everything for easy access and a smoother cooking process. This approach saves time, reduces frustration, and ensures you're only doing what's essential to create a great meal. Similarly, Lean helps organizations 'declutter' their processes, making them more agile and responsive.

How the field grew

1930Origins in Toyota Production System (TPS) in Japan, pioneered by Taiichi Ohno and Eiji Toyoda.
1950TPS evolves, focusing on eliminating waste (muda), overburden (muri), and unevenness (mura).
1980Western companies begin studying TPS, recognizing its potential for efficiency and quality.
1990The term 'Lean' is coined by researchers at MIT to describe TPS principles in their book 'The Machine That Changed the World'.
2000Lean principles expand beyond manufacturing to healthcare, services, and IT, becoming a universal approach to operational excellence.
