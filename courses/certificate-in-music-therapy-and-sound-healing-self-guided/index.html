What is this field?

Music therapy and sound healing explore how structured sound and musical interventions can profoundly affect human health. This program delves into the scientific basis of these practices, examining their influence on brain function, emotional states, and physical well-being, particularly in contexts like end-of-life care and managing conditions such as Alzheimer's disease.

Think of it like tuning an instrument: just as specific frequencies and rhythms can bring harmony to music, targeted sound and musical approaches can help bring balance and healing to the body and mind. It's about understanding how these auditory experiences can be intentionally applied to support therapeutic goals.

How the field grew

1940sPost-World War II, music was widely used in hospitals to aid in the rehabilitation of service members.
1950The National Association for Music Therapy (NAMT) was founded, formalizing the profession.
1970sInterest in sound healing as a distinct, complementary therapeutic modality began to grow.
1998The American Music Therapy Association (AMTA) was formed, uniting the field's professional bodies.
2000sIncreased scientific research expanded understanding of music's neurological and psychological effects.
