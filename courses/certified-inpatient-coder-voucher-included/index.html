What is this field?

A Certified Inpatient Coder (CIC) is a specialized medical coding professional responsible for accurately translating complex medical documentation from hospital inpatient stays into standardized alphanumeric codes. These codes, primarily from the ICD-10-CM (diagnosis) and ICD-10-PCS (procedure) systems, are critical for billing, data analysis, research, and ensuring proper reimbursement for healthcare services.

Think of an Inpatient Coder as a translator for healthcare. When a patient stays in a hospital, every diagnosis, procedure, and service is meticulously documented by doctors and nurses. The coder reads these detailed records and converts them into a universal language of codes, much like a librarian categorizes books with specific numbers so they can be easily found and understood by anyone in the system.

How the field grew

1979The International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-9-CM) was introduced in the U.S. for coding diagnoses and procedures.
2008The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) announced the transition to ICD-10-CM and ICD-10-PCS, a more detailed coding system.
2013AAPC introduced the Certified Inpatient Coder (CIC) credential to validate expertise in inpatient medical record review and coding.
2015The U.S. officially transitioned from ICD-9-CM to ICD-10-CM and ICD-10-PCS for all healthcare transactions.
