What is this field?

Guided reading is a powerful instructional approach where teachers work with small groups of students who share similar reading development needs. The goal is to provide targeted support and instruction, helping students develop strategies for processing text, comprehending meaning, and becoming independent, fluent readers. It's a dynamic process that adapts to each group's specific challenges and strengths, fostering a love for reading and building critical literacy skills.

Think of it like a personal trainer for reading. Instead of a one-size-fits-all workout, a guided reading session is tailored to a small group's current fitness level and goals. The teacher provides just enough support—a 'spot'—to help students tackle challenging texts, learn new reading 'muscles,' and build confidence, gradually reducing assistance as they grow stronger and more independent in their reading journey.

How the field grew

1980sEmergence of balanced literacy approaches, emphasizing explicit instruction alongside authentic reading experiences.
1990sIrene Fountas and Gay Su Pinnell popularize and systematize guided reading as a core component of literacy instruction.
2000sIncreased focus on differentiated instruction and evidence-based reading practices, further integrating guided reading into classrooms.
PresentContinued evolution with digital tools and a focus on equity, ensuring all students receive targeted reading support.
