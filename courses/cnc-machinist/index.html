What is this field?

A CNC Machinist operates and programs Computer Numerical Control (CNC) machines, which are automated tools used to precisely cut, shape, and form materials like metal, plastic, and composites. These skilled professionals translate digital designs into physical products, playing a critical role in manufacturing everything from aerospace components to medical devices and consumer goods. They ensure accuracy, efficiency, and quality in high-volume production environments.

Think of a CNC Machinist as a digital sculptor, using advanced computer programs to guide powerful machinery with extreme precision. Instead of chiseling by hand, they orchestrate complex movements through code, transforming raw materials into intricate parts with unparalleled consistency and speed.

How the field grew

1940sEarly numerical control (NC) systems developed, laying the groundwork for automated machining.
1970sThe advent of microprocessors leads to Computer Numerical Control (CNC) machines, integrating computers for enhanced programming and control.
1990sWidespread adoption of CAD/CAM software streamlines design-to-manufacturing workflows, boosting CNC capabilities.
PresentModern CNC machining integrates advanced robotics, multi-axis capabilities, and Industry 4.0 principles for smart manufacturing.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Foundations of Machining & Safety

Production floor safety, metal cutting principles, material science, and workholding techniques.

02
Grinding Processes & Abrasives

Grinding safety, surface grinder operation, grinding wheel materials, and dressing techniques.

03
CNC Machine Fundamentals

Introduction to CNC lathes and mills, coordinate systems, and basic G-code programming.

04
Advanced CNC Programming & Controls

Detailed programming for Fanuc, Haas, and Mazak controls, offsets, and program execution.

05
Precision Inspection & Quality Assurance

Blueprint reading, GD&T, measurement tools, calibration, and in-line inspection methods.

06
Lean Manufacturing & Process Improvement

Six Sigma, 5S, Lean principles, Total Productive Maintenance, and continuous improvement strategies.

07
Manual Machining Techniques

Basics of manual mill and engine lathe operation, setup, and holemaking.
