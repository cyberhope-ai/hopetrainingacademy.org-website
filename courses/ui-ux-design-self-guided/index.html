What is this field?

UI/UX design focuses on creating digital products that are both visually appealing (User Interface) and easy to use, efficient, and enjoyable (User Experience). It's about understanding user needs and behaviors to craft interfaces that are intuitive and effective, ensuring a seamless interaction between people and technology.

Think of it like designing a house. UI is the interior decoration – the paint colors, furniture, and lighting that make it look good. UX is the architectural blueprint – how the rooms flow, where the stairs are placed, and how easy it is to move around and live comfortably within the space. Both are crucial for a great home, just as they are for a great digital product.

How the field grew

1980sEarly concepts of human-computer interaction (HCI) emerge, focusing on user-friendly systems.
1990sDon Norman coins the term 'User Experience' while working at Apple, emphasizing the holistic user journey.
2000sThe rise of web and mobile applications drives increased demand for user-centered design principles.
2010sUX becomes a distinct and highly sought-after profession, integrating research, design, and testing methodologies.
2020sAI, virtual reality, and immersive technologies further expand the scope and complexity of UI/UX design.

What the career pays

UI/UX Designers are responsible for enhancing user satisfaction by improving the usability, accessibility, and pleasure provided in the interaction between the user and a product. They conduct research, design wireframes and prototypes, and test interfaces to ensure digital products meet both user needs and business goals.
