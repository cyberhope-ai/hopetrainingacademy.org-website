What is this field?

Human Anatomy and Physiology (A&P) is the fundamental study of the human body's structure and how its various parts function. This program introduces you to systemic anatomy, exploring the physical makeup of organ systems, and physiology, explaining how these structures work. You'll gain a comprehensive understanding of the body's intricate design and operational mechanisms.

Think of the human body as an incredibly complex, self-regulating city. Anatomy is like studying the city's infrastructure – its buildings, roads, and utilities – understanding where everything is located. Physiology is like studying how that city operates – how traffic flows, how power is generated and distributed, and how waste is managed. This program teaches you both the 'blueprint' and the 'operations manual' of the human body.

How the field grew

Ancient Egypt (c. 1600 BCE)Early medical texts, like the Ebers Papyrus, describe organs and basic physiological functions.
Ancient Greece (c. 400 BCE)Hippocrates and Galen lay foundational principles for scientific medicine and systematic anatomical study.
Renaissance (16th Century)Andreas Vesalius publishes 'De humani corporis fabrica,' revolutionizing anatomical understanding through detailed dissections and illustrations.
17th CenturyWilliam Harvey accurately describes the circulatory system, advancing physiological knowledge of blood flow and the heart.
Modern Era (20th-21st Century)Advances in microscopy, genetics, and medical imaging deepen understanding of cellular processes and complex systemic interactions.
