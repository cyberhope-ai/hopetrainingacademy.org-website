What is this field?

A Human Resources Manager is the strategic backbone of any organization's most valuable asset: its people. They are responsible for overseeing all aspects of employment, from recruiting and onboarding new talent to managing employee relations, compensation, benefits, training, and compliance with labor laws. Their work ensures a productive, fair, and legally sound workplace, directly impacting company culture and overall success.

Think of a Human Resources Manager as the conductor of an orchestra. While each musician (employee) plays a vital role, the conductor ensures everyone is in harmony, following the score (company policies and laws), and performing at their best. They manage the rhythm of hiring, resolve conflicts, ensure fair compensation, and help individual talents grow, all while keeping the entire ensemble aligned with the organization's strategic goals.

How the field grew

Early 20th CenturyThe rise of 'Personnel Management' focused on administrative tasks like payroll and record-keeping, driven by industrialization.
Mid-20th CenturyShift towards 'Human Relations,' recognizing the importance of employee morale, motivation, and well-being, influenced by behavioral science.
Late 20th CenturyEvolution into 'Human Resources Management,' emphasizing HR's strategic role in organizational success, talent development, and legal compliance.
1976The Human Resource Certification Institute (HRCI) is established, offering the Professional in Human Resources (PHR) certification to validate expertise.
2014The Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM) introduces the SHRM-CP (Certified Professional) certification, further professionalizing the field.
