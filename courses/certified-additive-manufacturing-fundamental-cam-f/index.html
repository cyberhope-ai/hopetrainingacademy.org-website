What is this field?

Additive manufacturing, often known as 3D printing, is a revolutionary process that creates three-dimensional objects by building them layer by layer from a digital design. Unlike traditional manufacturing methods that remove material (subtractive manufacturing), additive manufacturing builds up material, leading to less waste and the ability to create complex geometries previously impossible.

Imagine building a sculpture by carefully adding tiny bits of clay until the full form emerges, rather than carving it out of a solid block. That's the essence of additive manufacturing: a precise, layer-by-layer construction that transforms digital blueprints into physical realities, opening doors to innovation in product design and production across countless industries.

How the field grew

1980sStereolithography (SLA) invented, laying the groundwork for modern 3D printing.
1990sKey technologies like Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) and Selective Laser Sintering (SLS) emerge.
2000sOpen-source 3D printing initiatives increase accessibility and innovation.
2010sIndustrial adoption accelerates with advancements in materials and metal 3D printing.
2020sAdditive manufacturing integrates further into supply chains and large-scale production.

Principles of optimizing designs specifically for 3D printing and digital file management.

AM as a secondary process, prototype to production, hybrid manufacturing, and reverse engineering.

Basic safety guidelines and critical quality considerations specific to 3D printing.
