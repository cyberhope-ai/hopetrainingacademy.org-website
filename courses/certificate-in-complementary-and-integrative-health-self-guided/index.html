What is this field?

Complementary and integrative health (CIH) refers to a broad range of practices and products that are not typically part of conventional medical care but are used alongside or in place of it. This includes approaches like acupuncture, massage, herbal remedies, mindfulness, and nutritional therapies, all aimed at treating the whole person—mind, body, and spirit.

Think of conventional medicine as a highly specialized mechanic for your car, expertly fixing specific engine problems. Complementary and integrative health, on the other hand, is like a comprehensive car care specialist who not only fixes issues but also optimizes fuel, improves tire pressure, and ensures all systems work harmoniously for peak performance and longevity. It's about enhancing overall well-being, not just treating symptoms.

How the field grew

1970sGrowing public interest in holistic health and alternative therapies emerges across the United States.
1992The U.S. National Institutes of Health establishes the Office of Alternative Medicine, recognizing the need for research into these practices.
2000sIntegrative medicine programs begin to appear in major academic medical centers, signaling broader acceptance.
2010sIncreased scientific research validates the efficacy of many complementary health approaches, leading to wider adoption.
