What is this field?

Civil litigation refers to the legal process of resolving disputes between individuals or organizations, typically seeking monetary damages or specific performance, rather than criminal penalties. It encompasses everything from contract disputes and personal injury claims to family law matters. Understanding this process is fundamental for legal professionals, especially paralegals, who play a crucial support role in every stage.

Think of civil litigation as a structured argument between two parties, overseen by a judge, to settle a disagreement without involving criminal charges. A paralegal acts as the legal team's navigator, preparing documents, conducting research, and managing case details to ensure the process moves smoothly and effectively towards a resolution, whether through settlement or trial.

How the field grew

1960sEmergence of the paralegal profession to support growing legal demands.
1970sFormalization of paralegal education and professional associations.
1980sIncreased reliance on paralegals in civil litigation to manage complex cases efficiently.
PresentParalegals are indispensable members of legal teams, especially in civil litigation, handling critical tasks from discovery to trial preparation.
