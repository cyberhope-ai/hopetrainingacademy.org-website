What is this field?

American Sign Language (ASL) is a complete, natural language with its own grammar and syntax, distinct from spoken English. It is the primary language of many North Americans who are Deaf or hard of hearing, and it is used by hearing individuals who communicate with them. Learning ASL opens doors to diverse communities and enriches your understanding of non-verbal communication.

Imagine being able to converse fluently without uttering a sound, expressing complex thoughts and emotions through precise handshapes, movements, and facial expressions. ASL offers this unique form of expression, allowing for direct and meaningful interactions with a vibrant cultural community. It's more than just a series of gestures; it's a living language with a rich history and cultural significance.

How the field grew

1817The American School for the Deaf, the first permanent school for the deaf in the U.S., is founded, laying groundwork for ASL.
1864Gallaudet University, the world's only university for Deaf and hard of hearing students, is chartered by President Lincoln.
1960sASL gains recognition as a distinct, fully developed language, separate from English, through linguistic research.
1965The first comprehensive dictionary of American Sign Language is published, solidifying its linguistic status.

What the career pays

Individuals proficient in American Sign Language facilitate communication between Deaf and hearing individuals in various settings, fostering understanding and inclusion. They can work in educational, medical, community, and business environments, bridging communication gaps.
