What is this field?

Accounts Payable (AP) is the financial process of managing money owed by a business to its suppliers and vendors. It involves receiving, verifying, and processing invoices, ensuring timely and accurate payments, and maintaining detailed financial records. An Accounts Payable Specialist is crucial for a company's financial health, preventing fraud, and maintaining good vendor relationships.

Think of it like managing your household bills, but for a large organization. Just as you track utility bills, rent, and credit card statements to pay them on time and accurately, an AP specialist handles hundreds or thousands of invoices from various suppliers, making sure every payment is correct, approved, and recorded.

How the field grew

1980sThe rise of personal computers begins to automate basic accounting tasks, moving AP away from purely manual ledger entries.
1990sEnterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems become more prevalent, integrating AP with other financial functions and increasing efficiency.
2000sIncreased regulatory scrutiny (e.g., Sarbanes-Oxley Act) emphasizes the importance of robust internal controls and fraud prevention in AP.
2010sCloud-based solutions and advanced automation technologies begin to transform AP processes, reducing manual intervention and improving data accuracy.
PresentAccounts Payable professionals are vital strategic partners, leveraging technology to optimize cash flow, manage risk, and contribute to business intelligence.

Do I need a degree to become an Accounts Payable Specialist?

While a degree can be helpful, it's not strictly required. The IOFM APS certification recommends a combination of education and prior AP experience (e.g., 3 years experience without a degree, or 1 year with a 4-year degree). This program helps you gain the knowledge for certification.
