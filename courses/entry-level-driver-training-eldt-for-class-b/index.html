What is this field?

The Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) Class B CDL Theory program is your crucial first step towards becoming a professional commercial truck driver. This program provides the foundational knowledge required by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) for individuals seeking a Class B Commercial Driver's License (CDL) for the first time. It covers critical topics from vehicle operation and safety protocols to advanced driving practices and non-driving responsibilities, ensuring you understand the rules and best practices of the road.

Think of it as learning the 'rules of the road' and the 'owner's manual' for large commercial vehicles before you ever get behind the wheel. While this program focuses on the classroom (theory) portion, it's a mandatory federal requirement that prepares you to confidently tackle your state's CDL exam and pursue the necessary behind-the-wheel training. Successfully completing this program means your training records are automatically submitted to the U.S. Department of Transportation's Training Provider Registry (TPR), clearing the path for your next steps.

How the field grew

2012Congress passed the Moving Ahead for Progress in the 21st Century Act (MAP-21), mandating the FMCSA to establish national entry-level driver training standards.
2016The FMCSA published the final rule for Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT), outlining comprehensive requirements for new commercial drivers.
2022The ELDT regulations became effective on February 7, 2022, requiring all new Class A, Class B, and passenger/school bus endorsement applicants to complete training from an approved provider.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Basic Operation

CMV controls, vehicle inspections, basic maneuvers, shifting, backing, and FMCSRs.

02
Safe Operating Procedures

Visual search, communication, distracted driving, speed/space management, night driving, and extreme conditions.

03
Advanced Operating Practices

Hazard perception, skid control, emergency maneuvers, and railroad-highway grade crossings.

04
Vehicle Systems and Reporting Malfunctions

Vehicle components, identification of malfunctions, roadside inspections, and maintenance.

05
Non-Driving Activities

Cargo handling, environmental compliance, hours of service, fatigue, post-crash procedures, and trip planning.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a college degree to enroll in this program?

No, a college degree is not required to enroll in the ELDT Class B CDL Theory program. A high school diploma or equivalent is generally sufficient for entry-level truck driving careers.

Does this program include an exam voucher for the CDL test?

This program focuses solely on the mandatory ELDT theory training. It does not include an exam voucher for the state-administered CDL knowledge or skills tests, nor does it cover the cost of behind-the-wheel training.

How long does it take to complete this training program?

This self-paced ELDT Class B CDL Theory program is designed to be completed in approximately 36 course hours, allowing you to learn at a pace that fits your schedule.

Does this program prepare me for a specific CDL exam version?

This program prepares you for the general knowledge requirements of the state-administered Class B CDL exam, as mandated by federal ELDT regulations. There are no specific 'versions' of the ELDT theory exam itself; it covers the foundational knowledge required by the FMCSA.

What kind of jobs can I get with a Class B CDL?

A Class B CDL opens doors to various driving roles, including delivery driver for local freight, dump truck operator, concrete mixer driver, utility truck driver, and refuse/recycling collector, among others.
