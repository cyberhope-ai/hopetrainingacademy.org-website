What is this field?

Constitutional Law is the body of law derived from the U.S. Constitution, defining the powers and limits of government and the fundamental rights of citizens. It serves as the supreme law of the land, governing everything from free speech and the separation of powers to the intricate structure of federal and state authority.

Think of the U.S. Constitution as the ultimate rulebook for how America operates. Constitutional Law is like understanding every intricate rule, amendment, and interpretation of that rulebook, ensuring fair play and upholding the fundamental principles upon which the nation was built. This knowledge is critical for navigating the complexities of the American legal system.

How the field grew

1787Constitutional Convention drafts the U.S. Constitution, establishing the framework for American governance.
1791The Bill of Rights is ratified, adding the first ten amendments and guaranteeing fundamental individual freedoms.
1803Marbury v. Madison establishes the principle of judicial review, empowering the Supreme Court to interpret the Constitution.
1868The 14th Amendment is ratified, introducing the Equal Protection and Due Process clauses that profoundly impact civil rights.
