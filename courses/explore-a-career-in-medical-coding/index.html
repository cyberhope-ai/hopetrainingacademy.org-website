What is this field?

Medical coding is the process of translating healthcare diagnoses, procedures, medical services, and equipment into universal alphanumeric codes. These codes are essential for accurate billing, insurance claims processing, and data analysis in the healthcare industry. Medical coders act as vital intermediaries, ensuring that patient care is correctly documented and reimbursed.

Think of medical coding like translating a doctor's notes into a secret language that computers and insurance companies understand. Just as a librarian organizes books by a specific cataloging system, medical coders organize complex medical information into standardized codes, making it efficient for healthcare systems to operate and communicate.

How the field grew

1948World Health Organization (WHO) publishes the first International Classification of Diseases (ICD) for global health statistics.
1960sDevelopment of the Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) system by the American Medical Association (AMA) for medical procedures.
1979ICD-9-CM (Clinical Modification) is introduced in the U.S. for coding diagnoses and procedures in hospitals.
2015United States transitions to ICD-10-CM/PCS, a significantly expanded and more specific coding system, marking a major shift in medical documentation.
