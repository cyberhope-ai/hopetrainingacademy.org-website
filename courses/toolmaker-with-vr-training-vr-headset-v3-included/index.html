What is this field?

A toolmaker is a highly skilled craftsperson who designs, builds, and repairs the specialized tools, dies, jigs, and fixtures used in manufacturing processes. These precision components are critical for producing everything from automotive parts to consumer electronics. This program equips you with the foundational knowledge and practical skills needed to excel in this demanding and rewarding field, including workholding, machining, materials science, and quality control.

Imagine a toolmaker as the 'engineer of the factory floor.' Just as a chef needs specialized molds to create intricate pastries, manufacturers need custom tools to shape raw materials into finished products. You'll learn to create these 'molds' and other essential equipment, ensuring precision and efficiency in production. Our program integrates virtual reality (VR) training, providing a safe, immersive environment to practice complex procedures and reinforce learning, much like a flight simulator prepares a pilot for real-world scenarios.

How the field grew

1798Eli Whitney introduces interchangeable parts, laying groundwork for modern manufacturing and tooling.
1880sStandardization of machine tool components begins, enhancing precision and efficiency.
1952First numerically controlled (NC) machine tool developed at MIT, revolutionizing machining accuracy.
1970sComputer Numerical Control (CNC) machines become widespread, integrating computers for greater automation.
2000sAdvanced CAD/CAM software and virtual reality training emerge, optimizing tool design and skill development.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Grinding Processes & Safety

Explore various grinding techniques, machine operations, fluid applications, and critical safety protocols.

02
CNC Machining & Programming

Learn CNC lathe and mill basics, G-code programming, CAD/CAM introduction, and control panel functions for Fanuc, Haas, and Mazak systems.

03
Precision Inspection & Quality

Master basic measurement, blueprint reading, Geometric Dimensioning & Tolerancing (GD&T), Lean manufacturing principles, and Six Sigma concepts.

04
Manual Machining & Workholding

Gain hands-on skills with manual mill and engine lathe operations, benchwork, layout, and advanced turning techniques.

05
Materials Science & Metal Cutting

Understand physical and mechanical properties of metals, classification of steel, heat treatment, cutting theory, and tool geometry.

06
Workplace Safety & Compliance

Cover OSHA regulations, PPE, lockout/tagout, hazard communication, fire safety, and ergonomics to ensure a safe work environment.
