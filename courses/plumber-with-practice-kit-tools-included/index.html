What is this field?

Plumbing is the system of pipes, drains, fittings, valves, and fixtures installed for the distribution of potable water, heating, and the removal of waterborne waste. It's a critical infrastructure component in every building, ensuring access to clean water and safe sanitation. Plumbers are the skilled professionals who design, install, maintain, and repair these vital systems, playing an indispensable role in public health and comfort.

Our comprehensive Plumber Training Program provides the foundational knowledge and hands-on experience you need to become a proficient plumber. Through detailed course materials, interactive exercises, and a complete practice kit with real materials and tools, you'll learn everything from pipe joining and water distribution to safety protocols and advanced system troubleshooting. It's like learning to build a complex puzzle, piece by piece, until you can construct and maintain entire water and waste systems with confidence.

How the field grew

2500 BCEEarly plumbing systems emerge in ancient civilizations like the Indus Valley and Roman Empire, utilizing clay pipes and aqueducts for water management.
1800sThe Industrial Revolution drives significant advancements in plumbing, with the introduction of cast iron pipes, flush toilets, and municipal water supply systems.
Early 1900sStandardized plumbing codes and licensing requirements begin to be established, professionalizing the trade and ensuring public health and safety.
Mid-20th CenturyNew materials like copper and various plastics (PVC, PEX) are introduced, offering greater flexibility, durability, and efficiency in plumbing installations.

What the career pays

Plumbers install, repair, and maintain pipes, fixtures, and other plumbing equipment used for water distribution and waste disposal in residential, commercial, and industrial settings. They ensure that water systems are safe, efficient, and compliant with all applicable codes and regulations.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a college degree to become a plumber?

No, a college degree is not typically required to become a plumber. This program provides the foundational knowledge and skills, and many successful plumbers enter the field through vocational training and apprenticeships, which are often preferred by employers.

Does this program include an exam voucher?

This program focuses on building comprehensive plumbing skills and knowledge for career entry. It does not include a specific exam voucher, as plumbing licensure requirements vary by state and typically involve a combination of experience and local examinations after initial training.

How long does it take to complete the Plumber Training Program?

This is a self-paced program designed for flexibility, totaling 120 course hours. You can complete it at your own pace, fitting it into your schedule, whether you finish in a few months or take longer.

What kind of job can I get after completing this program?

Upon successful completion, you will possess the specialized skills and technical knowledge to pursue entry-level positions as a plumber's apprentice or helper. This program provides an excellent foundation to begin a career in residential, commercial, or industrial plumbing, and to pursue a registered apprenticeship.

What is included in the practice kit?

The program includes a comprehensive 59-piece practice kit with real PVC, PEX, copper, ABS, and galvanized materials, along with essential plumbing tools. This allows for hands-on training exercises to solidify your learning.
