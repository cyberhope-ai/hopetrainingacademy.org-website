How the field grew

1950sRise of preventative maintenance strategies in industrial sectors.
1980sIntegration of Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) in automation.
2000sWidespread adoption of Lean and Six Sigma methodologies for efficiency.
2010sIncreased reliance on robotics and advanced automation in manufacturing.
2020sEmergence of Virtual Reality (VR) for immersive technical skills training.

What the career pays

Maintenance Technicians are crucial for keeping industrial equipment and facilities operational, performing diagnostics, repairs, and preventative care. They ensure machinery functions reliably, contributing directly to productivity and safety.

$33,260entry
$52,610median
$84,280experienced

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment Statistics, Maintenance and Repair Workers, General (49-9071)

Jobs it opens

Maintenance Technician

Performs routine and emergency repairs on industrial equipment.

Industrial Mechanic

Installs, maintains, and repairs factory machinery and mechanical equipment.

Equipment Engineering Technician

Supports engineers in maintaining and improving complex manufacturing systems.

Facilities Maintenance Specialist

Oversees the upkeep and repair of building systems and infrastructure.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Foundational Safety & Measurement

Workplace safety, basic measurement, blueprint reading, and calibration fundamentals.

02
Electrical Systems & Controls

Circuitry, power sources, electrical print reading, and safety for electrical work.

03
Mechanical Systems & Power Transfer

Forces of machines, power transmission components, bearings, and lubrication.

04
Fluid Systems: Hydraulics & Pneumatics

Fluid power principles, component identification, control valves, and preventative maintenance.

05
Machining & Grinding Processes

Manual mill and lathe operations, grinding theory, safety, and wheel selection.

06
Quality & Process Improvement

Lean manufacturing, Six Sigma, 5S, Total Productive Maintenance, and troubleshooting.

07
Inspection & Materials

Tolerance, GD&T, advanced inspection methods, and material properties.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a degree to enroll in this program?

No, this program is designed for individuals seeking to enter or advance in the maintenance technician field, regardless of prior degree attainment. It focuses on practical skills and knowledge.

Does this program include an exam voucher for a certification?

This program focuses on building comprehensive skills for a maintenance technician career and does not include an exam voucher for a separate industry certification.

How long will it take to complete the Maintenance Technician program?

This program consists of 260 course hours and is self-paced, allowing you to complete it on a schedule that fits your life and commitments.

Does this program prepare me for a specific certification exam?

This program provides a robust foundation in the knowledge and skills required for a maintenance technician role, validating worker competencies rather than preparing for a single, specific external certification exam.

What kind of job can I get after completing this program?

Upon completion, you will be prepared for roles such as Maintenance Technician, Industrial Mechanic, Equipment Engineering Technician, or Facilities Maintenance Specialist in various industrial and manufacturing settings.
