What is this field?

The Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) is a globally recognized credential for information security leaders. It validates your expertise across a broad spectrum of cybersecurity domains, demonstrating your ability to design, implement, and manage enterprise-level security programs. This program is designed for experienced security practitioners aiming for advanced roles.

Think of a CISSP as the architect and general contractor for a highly secure building. They don't just put up walls; they design the entire security system from the ground up, ensuring every door, window, and access point is protected, and that the building's operations are resilient against any threat. They understand the big picture of security, from policy to technical implementation, ensuring an organization's most valuable assets are safeguarded.

How the field grew

1989The International Information Systems Security Certification Consortium, (ISC)², was founded to address the need for a standardized cybersecurity certification.
1994The CISSP certification was launched, becoming one of the first vendor-neutral credentials for information security professionals.
2000sCISSP gained widespread industry recognition, becoming a benchmark for senior-level cybersecurity expertise globally.
OngoingThe CISSP Common Body of Knowledge (CBK) is regularly updated to reflect the evolving threat landscape and technological advancements.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a college degree to get CISSP certified?

While a degree can waive one year of experience, the CISSP certification primarily requires a minimum of five years of cumulative, paid, full-time work experience in at least two of the eight CISSP domains.
