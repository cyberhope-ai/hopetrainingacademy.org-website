What is this field?

Stock options are versatile financial instruments that give you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell a stock at a specific price by a certain date. They are powerful tools for managing risk, generating income, and speculating on market movements, offering flexibility beyond simply buying and selling shares.

Think of stock options like an insurance policy or a down payment on a future purchase. Just as you might buy car insurance to protect against unexpected costs, options can protect your investments from market downturns. Or, like putting a deposit down to buy a house later at a set price, options give you the right to buy or sell a stock at a set price, allowing you to lock in potential gains or limit losses.

How the field grew

1973The Chicago Board Options Exchange (CBOE) opened, standardizing options contracts.
1973The Black-Scholes model for option pricing was published, revolutionizing valuation.
1980s-PresentElectronic trading platforms made options more accessible to individual investors.
