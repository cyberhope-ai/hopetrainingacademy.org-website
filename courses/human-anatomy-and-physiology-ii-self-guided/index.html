What is this field?

Human Anatomy and Physiology II builds upon your foundational knowledge from A&P I, delving deeper into the complex structures and functions of the human body. This course explores the microscopic world of tissues (histology), the intricate mechanisms of our senses, the vital chemical reactions that power our cells (cellular metabolism), and the significant biological changes that occur throughout the entire human lifespan, from prenatal development to late adulthood. It's like understanding not just the parts of a sophisticated machine, but how each gear, wire, and circuit works together, and how it changes over its operational lifetime.

This comprehensive training is essential for anyone pursuing careers in healthcare, nursing, allied health, or medical research. By mastering these advanced concepts, you gain a critical understanding of how the body maintains health, responds to disease, and develops over time, providing a robust scientific basis for clinical practice and further academic pursuits.

How the field grew

1543Andreas Vesalius publishes 'De humani corporis fabrica,' revolutionizing anatomical study.
1628William Harvey accurately describes the circulatory system, a cornerstone of physiology.
1839Theodor Schwann and Matthias Schleiden propose the cell theory, foundational to understanding life.
1953Watson and Crick describe the double helix structure of DNA, unlocking cellular mechanisms.
PresentOngoing advancements in genomics, imaging, and molecular biology continue to refine our understanding of human physiology.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Histology Fundamentals

Explore the four primary tissue types, their structures, functions, and organization within organs.

02
The Senses

Understand the anatomy and physiology of vision, hearing, balance, taste, and smell.

03
Cellular Metabolism

Investigate energy production, nutrient utilization, and waste elimination at the cellular level.

04
Body Chemistry & Regulation

Examine key organic and inorganic chemicals, their roles, and homeostatic regulation.

05
Human Lifespan Development

Trace biological changes from prenatal development through infancy, childhood, adolescence, and adulthood.

06
Aging and Gerontology

Analyze physiological changes associated with aging and common health challenges in late adulthood.
