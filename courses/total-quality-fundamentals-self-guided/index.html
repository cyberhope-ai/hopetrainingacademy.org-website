What is this field?

Total Quality Management (TQM) is a holistic approach to long-term success through customer satisfaction. It involves all members of an organization in improving processes, products, services, and the culture in which they work. TQM emphasizes continuous improvement, data-driven decision-making, and a focus on preventing defects rather than just detecting them.

Think of TQM like a master chef meticulously perfecting a recipe. It's not just about fixing a burnt dish (defect detection); it's about ensuring every ingredient is fresh, every step is precise, and every team member understands their role in creating a consistently delicious meal (process improvement and prevention) that delights the customer.

How the field grew

1950sW. Edwards Deming and Joseph Juran introduce quality control principles to post-war Japan, leading to significant industrial resurgence.
1980sTQM gains widespread adoption in Western industries, driven by global competition and the success of Japanese manufacturing.
1987The Malcolm Baldrige National Quality Award is established in the U.S., promoting excellence in quality management practices across various sectors.
