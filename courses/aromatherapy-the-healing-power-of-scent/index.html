What is this field?

Aromatherapy is a holistic practice that harnesses the natural aromatic compounds, known as essential oils, extracted from plants to promote physical and psychological well-being. It involves understanding the unique properties of various oils, their historical applications, and their impact on the body and mind through inhalation or topical application. This program delves into the science behind these potent plant extracts, exploring how they interact with our systems to support health.

Think of aromatherapy like a carefully curated botanical library, where each 'book' (essential oil) holds unique stories of healing and comfort. Just as a librarian guides you to the right book for your needs, an aromatherapist learns to select and blend essential oils to address specific wellness goals, whether it's calming the mind, invigorating the body, or supporting natural healing processes.

How the field grew

Ancient CivilizationsEarly use of aromatic plants for medicinal, spiritual, and cosmetic purposes in Egypt, China, and India.
10th CenturyPersian physician Avicenna refines the distillation process, making essential oil extraction more efficient.
Early 20th CenturyFrench chemist René-Maurice Gattefossé coins the term 'aromatherapy' after discovering lavender oil's healing properties for burns.
Mid-20th CenturyAromatherapy gains traction in Europe as a complementary therapy, particularly in France and the UK.
