What is this field?

This professional development program equips educators with the essential strategies to cultivate a growth mindset in their gifted and talented students. You'll explore the profound benefits of this perspective, which emphasizes that abilities and intelligence can be developed through dedication and hard work, rather than being fixed traits. The course provides practical tools and resources designed specifically to support these high-achieving learners in overcoming challenges and embracing continuous improvement.

Think of it like teaching a child to ride a bike. A fixed mindset might say, 'I can't do it, I'll never learn.' A growth mindset, however, encourages them to say, 'I haven't learned it *yet*, but with practice, I will.' This program helps you instill that 'yet' mentality in gifted students, guiding them to see difficulties as opportunities for growth and to value effort over innate talent, fostering a lifelong love of learning and resilience.

How the field grew

1970sEarly psychological research by Carol Dweck on achievement motivation and self-theories begins to lay the groundwork for mindset concepts.
1988Dweck and Leggett publish 'A Social-Cognitive Approach to Motivation and Personality,' formally introducing the fixed and growth mindset framework.
2006The publication of Carol Dweck's book 'Mindset: The New Psychology of Success' popularizes the concept, bringing it into mainstream education.
2010sWidespread adoption and integration of growth mindset principles into educational curricula and teacher training programs globally.
