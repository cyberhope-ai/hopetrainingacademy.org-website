What is this field?

English Language Learner (ELL) Vocabulary Strategies refer to specific instructional techniques and approaches designed to help students who are learning English acquire and master new words. These strategies are crucial because vocabulary knowledge is a strong predictor of reading comprehension and overall academic success for ELL students, who often face the dual challenge of learning a new language while simultaneously learning academic content.

Imagine vocabulary as the building blocks of language. For ELL students, it's like trying to build a house with some blocks missing or without knowing what each block does. This training provides educators with a toolkit of methods to help these students identify, understand, and use these essential building blocks, ensuring they can construct strong foundations for their learning across all subjects.

How the field grew

1968The Bilingual Education Act was passed, marking the first major federal legislation to address the needs of English Language Learners in schools.
1980sGrowing recognition of the importance of academic language led to the development of sheltered instruction and content-based ESL approaches.
2002The No Child Left Behind Act mandated accountability for the academic progress of English Language Learners, including language proficiency.
2015The Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) replaced NCLB, continuing to emphasize support for English learners and their language development.
PresentCurrent pedagogical practices emphasize explicit, systematic, and tiered vocabulary instruction tailored to the diverse needs of ELL students.
