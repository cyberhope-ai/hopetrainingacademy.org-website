What is this field?

Action research in the classroom is a systematic, cyclical process that empowers educators to investigate and improve their own teaching practices and student learning outcomes. It involves identifying a problem, collecting data, analyzing findings, taking action based on those findings, and then reflecting on the results to inform future steps. This hands-on approach transforms teachers into proactive researchers, continuously refining their instructional methods.

Think of it like a scientist in your own classroom. Instead of just teaching, you're also observing, experimenting, and collecting evidence to see what truly works best for your students. This course provides you with the framework and tools to conduct these 'experiments' effectively, ensuring your teaching is always evolving and improving based on real-world data from your unique learning environment.

How the field grew

1940sPsychologist Kurt Lewin coins the term 'action research,' emphasizing its cyclical nature of planning, acting, observing, and reflecting.
1970sAction research gains traction in education, empowering teachers to engage in systematic inquiry within their own classrooms.
2000sThe rise of data-driven instruction solidifies action research as a critical tool for evidence-based teaching and continuous improvement.
PresentAction research remains essential for reflective practitioners seeking to enhance student engagement, learning, and overall educational effectiveness.
