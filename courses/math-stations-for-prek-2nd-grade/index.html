What is this field?

Math stations are dynamic learning centers where young students (PreK-2nd grade) engage in hands-on, differentiated math activities. Instead of a single teacher-led lesson, students rotate through various stations, each focusing on a different math concept or skill. This approach allows for personalized learning, caters to diverse learning styles, and provides opportunities for collaborative problem-solving and independent practice.

Think of math stations like a well-organized learning playground for numbers and shapes. Each 'play area' offers a unique way to interact with math concepts, from building with blocks to counting games or solving puzzles. This method keeps young minds active and curious, making math accessible and enjoyable for every child in your classroom.

How the field grew

Early 1900sProgressive Education Movement emphasizes active, hands-on learning for children.
Mid-20th CenturyPiaget's theories on cognitive development highlight stage-based learning in children.
1980sRise of Constructivism in education, advocating for students to build their own knowledge.
2000sIncreased focus on Differentiated Instruction, tailoring education to individual student needs.
