What is this field?

A Residential Electrician is a skilled tradesperson responsible for installing, maintaining, and repairing electrical systems in homes and other residential structures. This involves everything from wiring new constructions and updating existing homes to troubleshooting electrical issues, ensuring safety, and adhering to strict building codes like the National Electrical Code (NEC). They work with various components, including outlets, switches, lighting fixtures, circuit breakers, and sometimes even renewable energy systems like solar panels.

Think of a residential electrician as the architect of a home's nervous system. Just as nerves carry signals throughout the body, electrical wires carry power to every corner of a house, enabling lights, appliances, and devices to function. This program equips you with the knowledge and practical skills to build, maintain, and diagnose this essential system, ensuring homes are safe, functional, and powered efficiently.

How the field grew

1897First National Electrical Code (NEC) published, establishing foundational safety standards.
Early 1900sWidespread adoption of electricity in homes drives demand for skilled electricians.
1970sIntroduction of Ground-Fault Circuit Interrupters (GFCIs) significantly enhances electrical safety in residential settings.
2000sGrowth in renewable energy systems, like residential photovoltaic installations, expands electrician's scope.
PresentOngoing advancements in smart home technology and energy efficiency continue to evolve the trade.

What the career pays

Residential Electricians install, maintain, and repair electrical power, communications, lighting, and control systems in homes. They ensure all work complies with the National Electrical Code (NEC) and local regulations, guaranteeing safety and functionality.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a college degree to become a Residential Electrician?

No, a college degree is not required. This program provides the foundational knowledge and skills needed for an entry-level career, preparing you for state-specific licensing requirements.

Does this program include an exam voucher for a certification?

This program prepares you for state electrical licensing exams, which are typically required to work as an electrician. However, it does not include a voucher for a specific certification exam.

How long does it take to complete the Residential Electrician training?

This self-paced program consists of 125 course hours. The actual time to complete it will vary based on your individual study schedule and commitment.

What licensing exam does this program prepare me for?

This program prepares you with the knowledge required for various state and local residential electrician licensing exams. Please verify specific requirements with your state or local licensing board, as rules vary.

What kind of job can I get after completing this training?

Upon completion, you will be prepared for entry-level roles such as a Residential Electrician Apprentice, Electrical Installer, or Maintenance Electrician, ready to contribute to residential construction and service projects.
