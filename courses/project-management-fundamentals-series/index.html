What is this field?

Project management is the art and science of planning, executing, and closing projects to achieve specific goals within a defined timeframe and budget. It involves guiding teams, managing resources, and mitigating risks to deliver successful outcomes, whether launching a new product, constructing a building, or implementing a new software system.

Think of a project manager as the conductor of an orchestra. They don't play every instrument, but they ensure each musician plays their part in harmony, keeping the rhythm, managing the tempo, and bringing the entire performance to a successful and impactful crescendo.

How the field grew

Early 20th CenturyHenry Gantt develops the Gantt chart, a foundational tool for project scheduling and resource allocation.
1950sThe Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT) and Critical Path Method (CPM) are developed, formalizing project planning and control.
1969The Project Management Institute (PMI) is founded, establishing professional standards and certifications for the field.
1980s-1990sThe rise of personal computing and specialized software tools transforms project management, enabling more complex planning and tracking.
2000s-PresentAgile and Lean methodologies gain prominence, emphasizing flexibility, iterative development, and rapid response to change in project execution.
