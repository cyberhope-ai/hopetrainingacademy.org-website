What is this field?

An optician is a vital eye care professional who helps patients select, fit, and dispense eyeglasses and contact lenses based on prescriptions from optometrists or ophthalmologists. They guide individuals through lens options, frame styles, and ensure proper fit and comfort, playing a crucial role in improving visual acuity and overall eye health.

Think of an optician as the expert stylist and technician for your eyes. Just as a tailor custom-fits clothing to your body, an optician custom-fits corrective eyewear to your unique vision needs and facial structure, ensuring optimal clarity and comfort for improved sight.

How the field grew

1971Myopia rates in the U.S. stood at 25%, indicating a baseline need for vision correction.
2017U.S. myopia rates surged to nearly 42%, highlighting a growing demand for skilled opticians.
2020Global nearsightedness affected a significant portion of the population, underscoring the widespread need for eye care professionals.
2030 (Projected)The World Health Organization projects 3.36 billion people worldwide will be nearsighted, increasing demand for opticians by 29%.

What the career pays

Opticians interpret prescriptions, guide patients in selecting appropriate eyewear, and ensure lenses and frames are precisely fitted for optimal vision and comfort. They are key members of the eye care team, directly impacting a patient's quality of life through improved sight.

Foundations of Opticianry

Role of an optician, eye anatomy and function, basic math, light principles, and ophthalmic lens design.

Spectacle Lenses and Frames

Understanding prescriptions, lens materials, treatments, frame types, adjustments, and measurements.

03
Advanced Optics and Dispensing

Prisms, decentration, keratometry, lensometry, effective power calculations, and multifocal considerations.

Contact Lens Science

Prefitting, types of contact lenses (soft, RGP, hybrid, scleral), and related conditions and pathology.

Common eye conditions, systemic diseases affecting eyes, slit lamp biomicroscopy, and industry laws and guidelines.

Strategies for the ABO and NCLE exams (NOCE/CLRE) and guidance for career advancement.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a college degree to become an optician?

No, a college degree is not required to become an optician. This program prepares you for the American Board of Opticianry (ABO) and National Contact Lens Examiners (NCLE) certification exams, which typically require a high school diploma or GED.

Does this program include an exam voucher for the certification exams?

This program provides comprehensive training to prepare you for the ABO and NCLE certification exams, but an exam voucher is not included in the course fee.

How long does it take to complete the Optician Certification Training program?

This self-paced program consists of 150 course hours, allowing you to learn at your own speed and complete the training based on your schedule and dedication.

Which certification exams does this program prepare me for?

This program thoroughly prepares you for the current American Board of Opticianry (ABO) certification exam and the current National Contact Lens Examiners (NCLE) exam, including the NOCE and CLRE components.

What kind of jobs can I get after completing this training?

Upon successful completion, you'll be prepared for entry-level roles such as Dispensing Optician, Apprentice Optician, or Optical Assistant in optical shops, optometrist offices, and ophthalmology clinics.
