What is this field?

Clinical Medical Assistants (CMAs) are vital members of healthcare teams, performing a blend of administrative and clinical duties to ensure smooth patient care. They are often the first and last point of contact for patients, playing a crucial role in patient experience and clinic efficiency. From preparing examination rooms and assisting with procedures to managing patient records and scheduling appointments, CMAs keep healthcare practices running effectively.

Think of a Clinical Medical Assistant as the central nervous system of a medical office. Just as the nervous system coordinates all bodily functions, a CMA coordinates the flow of patients, information, and clinical support, ensuring that doctors and nurses can focus on diagnosis and treatment while patients receive comprehensive, compassionate care.

How the field grew

1950sFormal training programs for medical assistants began to emerge, recognizing the need for skilled support staff in growing medical practices.
1961The American Association of Medical Assistants (AAMA) was founded, establishing professional standards and advocacy for the role.
1963The first national certification examination for medical assistants was introduced, formalizing the credentialing process.
1970s-PresentThe role expanded significantly with the rise of outpatient care and managed healthcare, making CMAs indispensable in diverse medical settings.

Jobs it opens

Medical Assistant

A broad term for professionals assisting healthcare providers in various settings.

Clinical Medical Assistant

Specifically focuses on patient care and clinical procedures within a medical office or clinic.

Certified Medical Assistant (CMA)

A credentialed professional who has passed a certification exam, demonstrating proficiency.

Registered Medical Assistant (RMA)

Another recognized credential for medical assistants, often through American Medical Technologists (AMT).
