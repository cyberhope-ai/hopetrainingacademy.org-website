What is this field?

A Teacher's Aide, also known as a paraprofessional educator or instructional assistant, plays a crucial role in supporting lead teachers and students within educational environments. These dedicated professionals assist with various duties inside and outside the classroom, including supervising students, reinforcing lessons, and providing guidance in studies. They often monitor student behavior, manage paperwork, and perform essential record-keeping tasks, helping to create a more effective and organized learning space.

Think of a Teacher's Aide as a co-pilot in the classroom, working alongside the main pilot (the teacher) to ensure a smooth and successful flight for all passengers (the students). They provide an extra set of hands and eyes, offering individualized attention and support that allows the lead teacher to focus on broader instructional goals, ultimately enhancing the learning experience for every student.

How the field grew

1965Elementary and Secondary Education Act (ESEA) increased federal funding, leading to more support staff in schools.
1975Education for All Handicapped Children Act (EAHCA) mandated inclusive education, increasing the need for aides to support students with disabilities.
2002No Child Left Behind Act (NCLB) required paraprofessionals in Title I schools to meet specific qualifications, including passing a standardized assessment.
Early 2000sThe ETS ParaPro Assessment was developed to help school districts meet federal requirements for paraprofessional qualifications.
