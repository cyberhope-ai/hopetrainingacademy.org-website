What is this field?

Ethical hacking is the practice of legally and systematically attempting to bypass the security of a computer system, application, or data to identify vulnerabilities that a malicious attacker could exploit. Think of it like hiring a professional safe-cracker to test your bank vault's security; they use the same tools and techniques as a criminal, but with your explicit permission and with the goal of making your assets safer, not stealing them.

This proactive approach helps organizations strengthen their defenses by understanding their weaknesses from an attacker's perspective. Ethical hackers employ a wide array of tools and methodologies, from reconnaissance and network scanning to exploiting system vulnerabilities and analyzing web application security, all to ensure that critical information assets are protected against real-world cyber threats.

How the field grew

1999EC-Council, the certifying body for CEH, was founded.
2003The Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) certification was first launched.
Early 2010sCEH gained ANSI accreditation, enhancing its industry recognition and credibility.
Late 2010sA practical exam component was introduced, requiring hands-on skill demonstration.
