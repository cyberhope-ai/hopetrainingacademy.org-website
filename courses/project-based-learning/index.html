What is this field?

Project-Based Learning (PBL) is a dynamic teaching method where students gain knowledge and skills by working for an extended period to investigate and respond to an authentic, engaging, and complex question, problem, or challenge. It moves beyond rote memorization, encouraging deep understanding through hands-on engagement with real-world issues.

Think of it like building a house instead of just reading a blueprint. In traditional learning, you might study architectural drawings and construction principles in isolation. With PBL, you'd actually design, plan, and construct a model house, encountering and solving real problems along the way, learning the principles by applying them directly.

How the field grew

Early 1900sPhilosopher John Dewey advocates for 'learning by doing,' laying foundational concepts for experiential education.
1960s-1970sPBL gains prominence in higher education, notably at McMaster University Medical School, as a method for training professionals.
1990s-2000sPBL is increasingly adapted and integrated into K-12 education, with organizations developing frameworks and resources for broader implementation.
2010s-PresentWidespread adoption of PBL across educational levels, emphasizing 21st-century skills, technology integration, and authentic learning experiences.
