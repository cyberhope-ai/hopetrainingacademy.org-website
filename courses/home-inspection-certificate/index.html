What is this field?

A home inspector is a trained professional who evaluates the condition of a residential property, identifying potential issues or defects that could impact its safety, value, or functionality. This critical role provides prospective homebuyers with an objective assessment of a property's major systems and components, from the foundation to the roof.

Think of a home inspector as a property detective. They meticulously examine a house, much like a doctor performs a thorough check-up, looking for hidden problems or areas that might need attention. Their detailed report empowers buyers to make informed decisions, negotiate repairs, or understand future maintenance needs, ensuring a sound investment.

How the field grew

1970sEmergence of formal home inspection services in the U.S. as consumer protection grew.
1980sDevelopment of professional associations and standards of practice for home inspectors.
1990sIncreased state-level regulation and licensing requirements for home inspectors across the nation.
2000sIntegration of advanced technologies like thermal imaging and drone inspections into the field.
PresentContinued evolution of standards, reporting tools, and specialized inspection services.

No, a college degree is not typically required to become a home inspector. This program focuses on practical skills and knowledge essential for the profession.
