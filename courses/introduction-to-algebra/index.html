What is this field?

Algebra is a fundamental branch of mathematics that uses symbols and letters to represent numbers and quantities in formulas and equations. It provides a powerful framework for solving complex problems, understanding relationships between variables, and making predictions across various disciplines, from science and engineering to finance and technology.

Think of algebra as a universal language for logic and problem-solving. Just as a chef uses a recipe to combine ingredients and create a dish, algebra provides a set of rules and tools to manipulate numbers and symbols, allowing you to 'cook up' solutions to challenges that might otherwise seem impossible to untangle.

How the field grew

c. 820 ADPersian mathematician Al-Khwarizmi writes 'The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing,' introducing systematic methods for solving linear and quadratic equations, giving rise to the term 'algebra' (from 'al-jabr').
16th CenturyItalian mathematicians like Cardano and Tartaglia develop methods for solving cubic and quartic equations, significantly expanding algebraic capabilities.
17th CenturyRené Descartes introduces coordinate geometry, famously linking algebra with geometry and laying groundwork for calculus and modern mathematics.
19th CenturyThe development of abstract algebra by mathematicians like Galois and Cayley generalizes algebraic structures, leading to fields like group theory and ring theory, crucial for modern cryptography and physics.
20th CenturyAlgebra becomes indispensable in computer science, data analysis, and engineering, forming the backbone of algorithms, data structures, and computational models that power the digital age.
