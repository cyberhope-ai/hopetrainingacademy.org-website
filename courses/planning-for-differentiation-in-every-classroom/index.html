What is this field?

Differentiated instruction is a teaching philosophy that recognizes and responds to the unique learning needs of all students in a classroom. Instead of a one-size-fits-all approach, it involves tailoring instruction, content, process, products, and learning environments to accommodate individual student differences in readiness, interests, and learning profiles. This ensures that every student is challenged appropriately and has the opportunity to succeed.

Think of it like a chef preparing a meal for guests with various dietary restrictions and preferences. A differentiated classroom doesn't serve the same dish to everyone; instead, the 'chef' (teacher) adjusts ingredients, cooking methods, and presentation to ensure each 'guest' (student) receives a nourishing and enjoyable learning experience that suits their individual needs, without compromising the overall quality of the meal.

How the field grew

1956Bloom's Taxonomy of Educational Objectives introduced, providing a foundational framework for categorizing learning goals and cognitive processes.
1970sGrowing awareness in educational psychology of diverse student learning styles and needs, prompting educators to consider varied instructional approaches.
1990sCarol Ann Tomlinson's foundational work significantly popularizes differentiated instruction as a systematic and practical teaching philosophy.
2000sNorman Webb's Depth of Knowledge (DOK) framework emerges, offering a valuable tool to analyze and ensure the cognitive rigor of tasks and assessments.
PresentModern educational practices emphasize integrating these established frameworks to create inclusive, equitable, and highly effective learning environments for all students.
