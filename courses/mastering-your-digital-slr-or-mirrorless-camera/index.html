What is this field?

Beyond simply pointing and shooting, mastering your digital SLR or mirrorless camera means understanding the intricate relationship between light, lens, and sensor. It's about taking control of every setting – from aperture and shutter speed to ISO and white balance – to capture the exact image you envision, rather than relying on your camera's automatic decisions.

Think of it like learning to drive a car with a manual transmission instead of an automatic. While automatic gets you from point A to point B, a manual gives you precise control over speed, power, and handling, allowing for a more nuanced and exhilarating driving experience. Similarly, mastering your camera's manual controls empowers you to express your unique artistic vision and capture truly stunning photographs.

How the field grew

1975First digital camera prototype developed by Kodak engineer Steven Sasson.
1991Kodak introduces the first commercially available digital SLR camera, the DCS 100.
2000sDigital photography becomes mainstream, leading to the widespread adoption of DSLRs and a decline in film camera use.
2008Panasonic introduces the Lumix G1, the world's first mirrorless interchangeable-lens camera, offering a more compact alternative to DSLRs.
2020sMirrorless camera technology continues to advance rapidly, often surpassing DSLRs in performance and market share.
