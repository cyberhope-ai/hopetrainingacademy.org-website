What is this field?

Genealogy is the study of families and the tracing of their lineages and history. It involves systematically collecting and analyzing historical records, oral traditions, and other evidence to establish kinship and identify the ancestors and descendants of a particular individual or family. This foundational training equips you with the essential skills to begin your own fascinating exploration.

Think of genealogy as detective work for your family's past. You'll learn to gather clues from various sources, piece together a complex puzzle, and ultimately reveal the rich narrative of where you come from, connecting generations through meticulous research and discovery.

How the field grew

1800sRise of genealogical societies and organized record keeping.
Early 1900sEmergence of professional genealogists and specialized research techniques.
Mid-20th CenturyIncreased public interest in family history and efforts to preserve historical records.
Late 20th CenturyDigital revolution begins, making records more accessible online.
21st CenturyIntegration of DNA testing with traditional genealogical research.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a degree to pursue genealogy?

No, a formal degree is not required to begin your journey in genealogy. This course provides the practical skills and knowledge you need to start researching your family history effectively.

Does this course include an exam voucher?

No, this 'Genealogy Basics' training program does not conclude with a certification exam, and therefore, no exam voucher is included.

How long does it take to complete this training?

This online training typically involves 24 hours of course content, designed to be completed over approximately six weeks at your own pace.

Is this course aligned with a current certification exam?

This 'Genealogy Basics' training is a skill-building program focused on practical research methods, not preparation for a specific certification exam.

What kind of job can I get after taking this course?

This course primarily equips you for personal family history research or as a foundational skill for roles in historical societies, archives, or libraries. While 'Genealogist' is not a common standalone occupation, the skills are valuable for related research and historical roles.
