What is this field?

Additive manufacturing, often known as 3D printing, is a revolutionary process that builds three-dimensional objects layer by layer from a digital design. Unlike traditional 'subtractive' manufacturing, which cuts away material from a larger block, additive manufacturing creates objects by adding material only where it's needed, leading to less waste and greater design flexibility.

This innovative approach is transforming industries from aerospace to healthcare, enabling the creation of complex geometries, customized products, and rapid prototyping. As the industry continues its rapid expansion, skilled professionals who understand its core principles, technologies, and applications are in high demand to drive innovation and efficiency.

How the field grew

1984Charles Hull invents Stereolithography (SLA), patenting the first 3D printing technology.
1988Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) and Selective Laser Sintering (SLS) technologies are patented, expanding the range of additive manufacturing methods.
2000sOpen-source projects like RepRap emerge, democratizing access to 3D printing and fostering innovation.
2010sAdditive manufacturing transitions from primarily prototyping to widespread industrial adoption for production across diverse sectors.

What the career pays

An Additive Manufacturing Technician operates and maintains 3D printing equipment, ensuring the quality and efficiency of the production process. They prepare digital designs, select appropriate materials, and perform post-processing to create finished products.
