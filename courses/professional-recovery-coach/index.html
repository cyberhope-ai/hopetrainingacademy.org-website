What is this field?

A Professional Recovery Coach is a trained guide who supports individuals on their journey to overcome addiction and achieve lasting sobriety. This role focuses on a strengths-based approach, helping clients reconnect with their inner joy, build resilience, and establish meaningful connections within their community. Recovery coaches empower individuals to identify their goals, develop coping strategies, and navigate the challenges of recovery with confidence and support.

Think of a recovery coach as a personal guide who helps someone navigate a challenging wilderness. Instead of just pointing out dangers, they equip the individual with tools, build their confidence, and help them discover their own inner compass to find their way to a healthier, more fulfilling life. They don't provide therapy, but rather practical, empathetic support to foster personal transformation.

How the field grew

1970sEmergence of peer support models in mental health and addiction recovery.
1980sGrowth of mutual aid groups and informal peer-led recovery initiatives.
2000sFormalization of 'recovery coaching' and 'peer specialist' roles in addiction services.
22010sIncreased integration of peer support and recovery coaching into mainstream healthcare.
2020sExpansion of professional training and online certification for recovery coaches.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a degree to become a Professional Recovery Coach?

No, a college degree is not required to enroll in this program or to work as a Professional Recovery Coach. A high school diploma or equivalent is generally sufficient, as the focus is on practical skills and experience.

Is an exam voucher included with this program?

An exam voucher is not included with this Professional Recovery Coach training program. Any certification fees would be separate from the course cost.

How long does it take to complete the program?

This program consists of 60 course hours and is self-paced, allowing you to complete it at your convenience. Most students finish within a few months, depending on their study schedule.

Does this program prepare me for a specific certification exam?

This program is certified by the International Association of Professional Recovery Coaches (IAPRC) and provides Continuing Education Units (CEUs) recognized by NAADAC and IC&RC. It prepares you for professional certification through the IAPRC, though state-level peer certifications may have additional requirements.

What kind of job can I get after completing this program?

Graduates can pursue roles such as Professional Recovery Coach, Peer Support Specialist, Addiction Support Worker, or Community Health Advocate. You'll be equipped to work in various settings, including recovery centers, community organizations, or private practice.
