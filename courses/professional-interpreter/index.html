What is this field?

A professional interpreter acts as a vital bridge, enabling clear and accurate communication between individuals who speak different languages. Unlike a translator who works with written text, an interpreter facilitates spoken communication in real-time, ensuring that messages, nuances, and intentions are fully understood by all parties. This role is crucial in various settings, from healthcare and legal environments to community services, where precise understanding can have significant impacts.

Our Professional Interpreter training program equips you with the specialized techniques and ethical framework needed to excel in this dynamic field. You'll learn to manage communication flow, develop critical listening and memory skills, and navigate multicultural interactions with confidence. Think of it like being a conductor for an orchestra of words, ensuring every instrument (language) plays in harmony so the audience (communicators) can fully appreciate the symphony (message).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a college degree to enroll in this program?

No, this program requires a high school education (or equivalent) and full verbal fluency in at least two languages. While a bachelor's degree is often cited for general interpreting roles, many public services positions prioritize specialized training and proven language proficiency, which this program provides.

Does this program include an exam voucher for a certification?

This program focuses on developing your interpreting skills and includes a spoken language fluency assessment as part of the course completion. It does not include a voucher for an external certification exam.

How long does it take to complete this Professional Interpreter program?

This self-paced program consists of 40 course hours. The actual time to complete it will vary based on your individual study schedule and commitment.

What kind of job can I get after completing this program?

Upon successful completion, you will be prepared for roles as a public services interpreter, working in community organizations, healthcare settings, social services, and other areas where language access is critical.

What is the spoken language fluency assessment mentioned in the prerequisites?

As part of the program, you will undergo a phone-based fluency assessment in your 'A' (native) and 'B' (second) languages. You must achieve at least a level 3 on a 1-5 scale in both languages to pass the course and qualify as an interpreter.
