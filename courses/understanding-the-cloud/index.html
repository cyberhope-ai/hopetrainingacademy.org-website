What is this field?

Cloud computing is the on-demand delivery of IT resources—like servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence—over the internet with pay-as-you-go pricing. Instead of owning and maintaining your own computing infrastructure, you can access services from a cloud provider like Amazon Web Services (AWS), Google Cloud, or Microsoft Azure, paying only for what you use.

Think of it like electricity: instead of building your own power plant to generate electricity for your home, you simply plug into the grid and pay for the power you consume. Cloud computing offers similar convenience, scalability, and cost-efficiency for digital resources, allowing individuals and businesses to innovate faster and operate more flexibly.

How the field grew

1990sEarly concepts of utility computing and Application Service Providers (ASPs) emerge, laying groundwork for cloud services.
2006Amazon Web Services (AWS) launches Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2) and Simple Storage Service (S3), marking the beginning of modern public cloud infrastructure.
2008Google introduces Google App Engine, expanding the Platform-as-a-Service (PaaS) model.
2010Microsoft officially launches Azure, intensifying competition and accelerating cloud adoption across enterprises.
2010s-PresentRapid growth of Software-as-a-Service (SaaS), hybrid cloud solutions, and serverless computing, making cloud a cornerstone of modern IT.
