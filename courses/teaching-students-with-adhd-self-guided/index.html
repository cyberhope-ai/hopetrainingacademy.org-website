What is this field?

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that interfere with functioning or development. For educators, understanding ADHD is crucial to creating an inclusive and effective learning environment where every student can thrive. This training equips you with the knowledge and tools to identify challenges and implement supportive strategies.

Imagine a student trying to focus on a single task in a busy classroom, but their mind is like a television constantly flipping through channels, unable to settle on one program. This course provides practical, evidence-based techniques to help you guide these students, helping them 'tune in' to learning. You'll learn how to adapt your teaching methods, manage classroom behaviors, and foster a positive atmosphere for students with ADHD.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a specific degree to take this training?

No, this training is designed for educators and support staff at various stages of their careers. While a teaching background is helpful, it is not strictly required.

Is an exam voucher included with this purchase?

No, this professional development course does not include an exam voucher as it is not tied to a specific certification exam.

How long does it take to complete this training?

This is a self-paced online training program. Completion time varies by individual, but most participants can finish the material within a few weeks, dedicating a few hours per week.

Does this training prepare me for a specific certification exam?

This course is a professional development offering focused on practical strategies for teaching students with ADHD, rather than preparing for a specific certification exam.

What kind of job can I get with this training?

This training enhances the skills of current educators (general or special education teachers) and prepares individuals for roles supporting students with special needs, such as inclusion specialists or educational support professionals.
