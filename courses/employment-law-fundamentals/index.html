What is this field?

Employment law is the comprehensive body of rules and regulations that govern the relationship between employers and employees. It encompasses critical areas from fair hiring practices and wage standards to workplace safety, anti-discrimination measures, and proper termination procedures. A thorough understanding of these laws is indispensable for any business owner, manager, or HR professional to ensure ethical treatment of staff, prevent costly legal disputes, and foster a compliant and positive work environment.

Consider employment law as the foundational rulebook for the professional arena. Much like a sports game requires clear regulations to ensure fair competition and prevent chaos, the workplace relies on employment laws to protect both the rights of workers and the responsibilities of employers. This framework creates a structured, equitable, and predictable environment for all participants, allowing businesses to thrive while safeguarding employee welfare.

How the field grew

1938The Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) established minimum wage, overtime pay, recordkeeping, and child labor standards.
1964The Civil Rights Act (Title VII) prohibited employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.
1990The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) outlawed discrimination against individuals with disabilities in employment and public accommodations.
1993The Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) provided eligible employees with unpaid, job-protected leave for specified family and medical reasons.
