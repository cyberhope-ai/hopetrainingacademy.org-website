What is this field?

The Gradual Release of Responsibility (GRR) Model is a highly effective instructional framework designed to move students from dependence to independence in their learning. It systematically shifts the cognitive load from the teacher to the student through a series of structured phases: 'I Do' (teacher models), 'We Do' (teacher and students collaborate), 'You Do Together' (students collaborate), and 'You Do Alone' (students work independently). This approach ensures that learners acquire new skills and knowledge with appropriate support before applying them autonomously.

Think of it like learning to ride a bike. Initially, the instructor ('I Do') demonstrates how to balance and pedal. Then, they might hold the seat while you try ('We Do'). Next, you might ride with training wheels or a friend helping ('You Do Together'). Finally, you ride completely on your own ('You Do Alone'). The GRR model provides this same scaffolded support in the classroom, building student confidence and competence step by step.

How the field grew

1930sLev Vygotsky introduces the concept of the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD), laying theoretical groundwork for scaffolding and guided learning.
1976Jerome Bruner further develops the concept of 'scaffolding,' where teachers provide temporary support to help learners master new tasks.
Early 2000sDouglas Fisher and Nancy Frey popularize and formalize the Gradual Release of Responsibility Model, outlining its four distinct phases.
2007Fisher and Frey publish 'Better Learning Through Structured Teaching: A Framework for the Gradual Release of Responsibility,' widely disseminating the model.

Inside the course, module by module

01
Introduction to the Gradual Release Model

Understanding the core principles and benefits of the GRR framework in education.

02
Phase 1: 'I Do' - Focused Instruction

Strategies for explicit modeling, direct instruction, and clear communication of learning objectives.

03
Phase 2: 'We Do' - Guided Instruction

Techniques for interactive teaching, questioning, and providing immediate feedback during collaborative learning.

04
Phase 3: 'You Do Together' - Collaborative Learning

Designing effective group activities, fostering peer-to-peer support, and managing cooperative learning environments.

05
Phase 4: 'You Do Alone' - Independent Practice

Developing meaningful independent tasks, promoting self-regulation, and assessing individual mastery.

06
Implementing GRR in Your Classroom

Practical application, lesson planning integration, and adapting the model for diverse learners and subjects.
