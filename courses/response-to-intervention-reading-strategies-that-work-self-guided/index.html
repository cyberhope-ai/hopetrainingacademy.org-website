What is this field?

Response to Intervention (RTI) is a multi-tier approach to the early identification and support of students with learning and behavior needs. The RTI process begins with high-quality instruction and universal screening of all children in the general education classroom. Struggling learners are provided with progressively more intensive interventions, and their response to these interventions is closely monitored.

Think of RTI like a tiered support system for a garden. Instead of waiting for a plant to completely wilt, you provide different levels of care. All plants get good soil and sunlight (Tier 1: universal instruction). If some still struggle, you might give them extra water or fertilizer (Tier 2: targeted interventions). If they still don't thrive, you might move them to a special spot with tailored nutrients (Tier 3: intensive, individualized support). RTI applies this proactive, data-driven approach to student learning, ensuring no child is left behind.

How the field grew

1970s-1980sEarly research on learning disabilities and the limitations of the 'discrepancy model' for identification.
1990sGrowing dissatisfaction with 'wait to fail' approaches and calls for early intervention strategies.
2004The Individuals with Disabilities Education Improvement Act (IDEA) formally introduces RTI as an alternative to the discrepancy model for identifying specific learning disabilities.
2000s-PresentWidespread adoption and refinement of RTI frameworks in schools across the United States, emphasizing data-driven decision-making and tiered support.
