What is this field?

Interior design is the art and science of enhancing the interior of a building to achieve a healthier and more aesthetically pleasing environment for the people using the space. It involves understanding human behavior, functional requirements, and aesthetic preferences to create environments that are both beautiful and practical.

Think of an interior designer as a conductor for a living space. Just as a conductor brings together different instruments to create a harmonious symphony, an interior designer orchestrates elements like furniture, lighting, colors, and textures to create a cohesive and functional environment that resonates with its occupants.

How the field grew

Late 19th CenturyEmergence of professional decorators, often associated with architects, as industrialization changed living spaces.
Early 20th CenturyThe term 'interior decorator' gains prominence, focusing on aesthetics and furnishings.
Mid-20th CenturyThe field evolves into 'interior design,' emphasizing functional planning, safety, and psychological impact, moving beyond mere decoration.
Late 20th CenturyIncreased focus on sustainability, accessibility, and technology integration in design practices.

What the career pays

Interior designers plan and supervise the design and arrangement of building interiors to create functional and aesthetically pleasing environments. They select color palettes, materials, lighting, and furniture to meet client needs and safety standards.
