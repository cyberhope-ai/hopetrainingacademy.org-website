What is this field?

Response to Intervention (RTI) is a multi-tier approach to the early identification and support of students with learning and behavior needs. The RTI process begins with high-quality instruction and universal screening of all children in the general education classroom. Struggling learners are provided with increasingly intensive levels of instruction and intervention matched to their needs.

Think of RTI like a health check-up system for learning. Just as a doctor might use different levels of care—from general wellness advice for everyone to specialized treatments for those with specific issues—RTI provides tiered support. It ensures that students who are falling behind in reading get the right level of help, at the right time, preventing small gaps from becoming big problems.

How the field grew

1970sEarly concepts of identifying and supporting students with learning difficulties begin to emerge in educational research.
1990sMulti-tiered systems of support (MTSS) gain traction as a framework for delivering differentiated instruction.
2004The Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) formally recognizes RTI as an alternative to the 'discrepancy model' for identifying learning disabilities.
PresentRTI is widely adopted across U.S. school districts as a best practice for early intervention and academic support in reading.
