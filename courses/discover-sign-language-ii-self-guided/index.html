What is this field?

Discover Sign Language II is an online program designed to elevate your American Sign Language (ASL) proficiency beyond the basics. This self-guided course expands your vocabulary, refines your grammatical understanding, and deepens your appreciation for Deaf culture, enabling more fluid and nuanced conversations.

Think of it as moving from basic sentence construction to crafting compelling narratives. Just as you learn to express complex ideas and emotions in a spoken language, this program guides you in mastering the expressive power of ASL, preparing you for richer interactions and a broader understanding of the Deaf community.

How the field grew

1817First permanent school for the deaf established in the United States, laying groundwork for ASL use.
1960sAmerican Sign Language formally recognized as a distinct, fully developed language.
1988Deaf President Now movement at Gallaudet University raises national awareness for Deaf rights and ASL.
2000sIncreased demand for ASL interpreters and greater integration of ASL into education and media.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a degree to enroll in this program?

No, a degree is not required to enroll in Discover Sign Language II. This course is designed to build upon foundational sign language skills.

Is an exam voucher included with this course?

An exam voucher is not included with the Discover Sign Language II online course.

How long will it take to complete this course?

This is a self-guided online course, allowing you to learn at your own pace. The time to completion will vary based on your individual study schedule.

Does this course prepare me for a specific certification exam?

Discover Sign Language II is a skill-building course focused on enhancing your ASL proficiency, not preparing for a specific certification exam. It serves as a strong foundation for future interpreter training.

What kind of jobs can I get after completing this course?

While this course provides advanced ASL skills, it's a foundational step towards roles like ASL Interpreter Assistant, Community Liaison for Deaf Services, or positions requiring ASL fluency in customer service or education support.
